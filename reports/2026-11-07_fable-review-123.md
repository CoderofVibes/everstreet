Reviewer: Claude Fable 5.1, from the brief `reports/2026-11-07_fable-review-123-brief.md`. Read-only:

## Verdicts

1. **Handover strip.** Holds: the three sales, their closing transactions, the three refusals and the
   snapshot all come from records whose digests verify (all five offline; the snapshot and the
   linked-revenue record also `--live`), and the screen shows the records' fields. Weaker than it
   reads in two places: every "former owner refused" transaction is a *companion* contract's refusal
   (the asset-key or allowance contract), never the business contract's, and the strip hides the
   receiver; and "Closed · block N" is the block of the post-close read, not the block of the close.
2. **Revenue by source.** Holds, and is the strongest of the three: the classification is conservative
   (an unread registration is never counted as independent), the reasons are stated, what isn't
   checked is stated, the route is gated to the owner and the live buyer (customers and strangers get
   404), the demo, the attack case and the testnet run agree with the code, and the buyer's projection
   starts from independent uses. Open points are wording ("27 of 27 attacks refused" while this case is
   a flag), the suite recorded at an uncommitted state, and a privacy question about naming linked
   customers to the buyer.
3. **Static copy.** Holds: built from the server's own projections, nothing private in the output
   (no keys, tokens, local paths, environment values or external calls; the only outbound links are
   explorer links), every route works under `vite preview` and under the `_redirects`/`vercel.json`
   fallbacks. It depends on a host that honours those rewrites; a plain static host would 404 on
   every deep link.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. Medium · Strip · the "former owner refused" calls are companions' refusals, shown without the receiver

What the screen says: on `/proof/ledgerline`, under "Sales of this business", each sale's cell
"Former owner after the close" reads `request refused · E_NOT_OWNER`, `grant refused · E_NOT_OWNER`,
`approve refused · E_NOT_OWNER`, each with a transaction link and the "Testnet receipt" badge. The page
is titled for the business contract `ledgerline-v3-3b`; a judge reads this as the business contract
refusing its former owner.

What the records say:

| Sale | Seller | Refused call found | Receiver | Chain status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | founder-v3s3 | `request` (v3-ext step 4) | `feed-key-v3s3` (asset-key companion, series 3) | **succeeded**; the contract returned `{"refused":"E_NOT_OWNER"}` |
| 2 | buyer-v3s3 | `grant` (v3-ext step 41) | `budget-v3s3` (allowance companion, series 3) | failed, panic `E_NOT_OWNER` |
| 3 | noor-v3s3 | `approve` (v3-s4 step 28) | `budget-v3s4` (allowance companion, series 4) | **succeeded**; returned `"E_NOT_OWNER"` |

None is `ledgerline-v3-3b` refusing. Sale 1's refuser is the series-3 asset-key, the companion the
Nov 7 attack review found vulnerable and that series 4 replaced. No record in `data/evidence/`
(including `attack-pairs-testnet.json`, whose "Former seller acts as owner" pair is the same `grant`
to `budget-v3s3`) contains the business contract itself refusing a former owner on testnet; that
refusal exists only as Everstreet's HTTP 404s and on the synthetic chain.

Where: `server/src/examples.ts:240-249` takes the first later transaction signed by the seller whose
error or app resolution matches `/E_NOT_OWNER/`, for any receiver; the receiver is carried
(`examples.ts:262`) but `web/src/pages/RecordedProof.tsx:144-159` shows only method and code.
`server/test/handover-strip.test.ts:26-32` pins the code and the hash, not the receiver, so the test
passes whichever contract refused.

Why it matters: a judge who clicks the Sale 1 or Sale 3 link sees a *successful* transaction on the
explorer under a cell that says "refused"; one who reads the receiver sees a contract other than the
one the page is about. Showing the receiver ("by feed-key-v3s3") and saying that the companion asked
the business who the owner is would make the claim exact. Recording one `owner_probe` by a former
owner against `ledgerline-v3-3b` (the demo's existing flow) would give the strip the refusal it
implies.

### 2. Medium · Strip · "Closed · block N" is the block of the read after the close, not the close

Sale 1 says "Closed · block 276581444". The same page's "Light-client proof" row says "The close at
block 276581443" (the closing receipt; the transaction is in 276581442, `light-client-close.json`).
For sales 2 and 3 the "closed" checkpoints were read 10 s and 8 s after the closing transaction
(`v3-ext-testnet.json` checkpoint `closed_generation_3`, `v3-s4-testnet.json`
`closed_generation_4`), so the gap is several blocks.

Where: `server/src/examples.ts:233-235, 258` uses the first checkpoint whose owner is not the seller
and its block. The doc comment at `examples.ts:208-209` ("the closing transaction and the first block
after it") promises more than the data holds. The badge under the number ("Testnet final read, block
N") is accurate for what it is; the label "Closed" above it is what misleads.

Why it matters: two different "close" blocks on one page, and an explorer check puts the transaction
in a third. Either label it "Owner after the close, read at block N" or take the block from the
closing transaction's receipt.

### 3. Medium (latent) · Strip · the close search breaks on a record with checkpoints before the seller's tenure

`handoversOf` finds the "closed" checkpoint as the first checkpoint whose owner differs from the
seller (`examples.ts:234`). That is right only when every checkpoint before the close has the seller
as owner. A record that spans two sales, or that starts with a checkpoint under the previous owner,
would, for its second sale, pick the record's first checkpoint: the *previous* owner shown as the
buyer and a pre-sale block shown as "Closed", silently. The three current records have one sale each,
so the screen is right today; nothing in `handover-strip.test.ts` covers the case. The brief lists
"several sales" as an edge to check; this is the one place it fails. Searching only checkpoints
after the closing step (by `at`, or by the step's position) fixes it.

### 4. Low · Revenue by source · the attack suite was recorded at an uncommitted state and the docket hides that

`data/evidence/attack-suite.json` has `"commit": "6f7b3f6…", "uncommitted": true`; the code for
"Seller pays its own business" landed in the next commit (43aee12). The docket (demo and static copy,
`web/src/pages/AttackDocket.tsx:163-165`) shows "Run at commit 6f7b3f6" with no uncommitted mark. A
judge who checks out 6f7b3f6 and runs `npm run attacks -- --check` gets 26 cases, not 27. At HEAD the
check passes (27/27, 5/5; run during this review). Re-recording at a clean commit removes the gap.

### 5. Low · Revenue by source · "refused" wording for a case that is a disclosure

The docket's headline reads "27 of 27 attacks refused" and the static landing says "Attacks on the app
and its contract rules, each refused" (`web/src/proofSite.tsx:72`), but this case's outcome is
"Flagged · registration paid by the seller": nothing was refused, the revenue was labelled. It is also
filed under group 8, "Evidence substitution", which it isn't. The attack itself is a direct edit of
the synthetic chain's entitlement (`scripts/attack-suite.ts`, `storage_payer = SELLER`), not the
seller's `sponsor_shell` through a route; fine for a fixture, but it exercises the classification
only, not the purchase path. Wording: "27 of 27 attacks refused or flagged", and a group of its own.

### 6. Low / question · Revenue by source · linked customers are named to the buyer, with Everstreet's private links

The buyer sees each linked customer's account id and its reason (`UseEconomicsPage.tsx:108-120`;
demo: `theo.near · registration paid by the seller's side`). For `registration_paid_by_seller` the
fact is public on chain (the entitlement's `storage_payer`), so naming is checkable. For `owner_here`
and `team_here` (`revenueSources.ts:67-68, 94-95`) the fact is Everstreet's own person-to-account
mapping, which the seller or a team member may never have made public; the buyer learns "account X
belongs to the seller / to a team member". Independent customers are never named, which is right.
Question for Tony: is naming linked customers to the buyer intended, and should the Everstreet-only
reasons name the account at all?

### 7. Low · Revenue by source · "Linked to the seller" when the viewer is the owner

The card says "Linked to the seller" whether or not there is a sale: the owner of a business with no
live deal (the testnet linked-revenue run; Ledgerline and Relaydesk in the demo) sees her own
sponsored customers "linked to the seller", and after a close the new owner's own account counts as
`owner_account`, so the buyer's test uses would be "linked to the seller". Wording only; "the owner's
side" would be true in every state.

### 8. Low · Evidence · the linked-revenue record's checkpoints carry no block

`scripts/linked-revenue-testnet.ts:94-102` records `control`, `entitlement` and `earlier_customer`
but no `block`, unlike every other record; `data/evidence/linked-revenue-testnet.json` has three
checkpoints with no height. `scripts/verify-rehearsal.ts:46` prints "3 checkpoints at final blocks"
regardless. The record still verifies and `--live` matches now, but it isn't pinned to a block, so
it can't be re-read at the moment it describes. Not shown on any screen.

### 9. Low · Static copy · deep links need a host that honours the rewrite files

`scripts/build-proof-site.ts:204-208` writes `_redirects` (Netlify, Cloudflare Pages) and
`vercel.json`. On a plain static host (GitHub Pages, S3 without rules, `python -m http.server`)
`/proof/ledgerline`, `/attacks` and `/example/…` return 404 on direct load and on reload; only `/`
works, and `vite preview` hides this because it falls back to `index.html` itself. `base` is `/`, so
the copy must also sit at a domain root. A `404.html` copy of `index.html` covers GitHub Pages; hash
routes would cover everything. The first `_redirects` line (`/data/* /data/:splat 200`) is a no-op.

### 10. Low · Static copy · the "every read has a file" test only scans three files for string literals

`web/test/proofSite.test.tsx:16` matches `fetch("/api/…")` in `RecordedProof.tsx`,
`AttackDocket.tsx` and `Showcase.tsx`. A read added through `api.ts`'s `call()`, a template string,
or a component imported from another file would pass the test and answer "Not part of this copy"
once hosted. Today all three pages use literals and the copy works; the guard is thinner than its
comment says.

### 11. Low · Revenue by source · two small edges

- `UseEconomicsPage.tsx:143-144`: the scenario field starts at `max(independent, 1)`, so a business
  whose only revenue is linked shows "Monthly uses: 1" over "Independent customers, last 30 days: 0".
- `useEconomics.ts:77`: a customer with charged uses on two plans has its registration read for the
  last plan only; `PAYERS_READ_MAX = 50` reads the first fifty customers by first use and leaves the
  rest "Registration not read" (stated on the screen, so honest).

## What was checked and found sound

- **Records.** `verify-rehearsal.ts` offline: `v3-direct`, `v3-ext`, `v3-s4`, `ledgerline-after-sales`,
  `linked-revenue` all OK. `--live`: the snapshot (owner ines-v3s4, generation 4; cara-v3s3-1 78
  unused, 22 used; code hash and zero access keys match) and the linked-revenue record (wren-v3s5 1
  unused, 1 used; `storage_payer` ines-v3s4) match testnet now. 78 + 22 = 100 bought, 0 retired.
- **Strip against records.** Sales 1 to 3 map to the `accept_sale` steps with resolution `closed`
  (direct step 30, ext step 39, s4 step 26); from/to accounts and generations 1→2→3→4 match the
  checkpoints; "80" at the first close is the direct record's `closed` checkpoint, the later runs
  never read the customer, shown as "Not read" rather than a number. A record whose digest is broken
  is dropped, and a record for another contract is ignored (tested; the test edits a copy, not the
  evidence). `ledgerline.json` in the static copy equals the server's projection.
- **Permissions.** `GET /api/sales/<signalwatch>/economics` over HTTP as delphine.near and buyer.near:
  200 with identical bodies; as cara.near, theo.near (the linked customer himself) and rosa.near: 404
  "No such sale" (`saleActions.ts:currentContext`, read from the contract at a final block). The
  page never reads the database for a public route; the static copy's bundle makes no call but the
  two data files (network log checked), and `/api/*` on it answers 404 `static` as the test says.
- **Demo numbers.** Signalwatch: 14 charged uses at 4 TUSD; 7 independent (28 TUSD, one customer),
  7 linked (theo.near, registration paid by delphine.near; `scripts/lib/demo/world.ts:99-107`);
  13 uses in the last 30 days of which 6 independent; the scenario starts at 6 and projects
  6 × 3.1 = 18.6 TUSD a month, 446.4 at 2×12. Totals add up.
- **Testnet run.** `linked-revenue-testnet.json`: `sponsor_shell` signed by ines-v3s4 (one chain
  transaction, hash recorded), the purchase by wren-v3s5, one charged use through the app, and the
  owner's economics recorded as 22 independent uses (Cara, 3.3 TUSD) and 1 linked (Wren, 0.15 TUSD,
  `registration_paid_by_seller`), 23 earned uses in total. The script stops if the contract doesn't
  name the owner as payer or if Cara were linked.
- **Classification code.** `revenueSources.ts`: reasons are a fixed allowlist; a `null` payer with no
  other link is "unchecked", never independent; the seller's own account as a customer, an operator,
  the owner's second account (`person_accounts`/`chain_accounts`), a team member's account and a
  seller-paid registration are each covered by `server/test/revenue-sources.test.ts`, including the
  no-price case (revenue `null`, not 0). `scenario.independent_uses_last_30_days` counts only the
  "independent" bucket (unchecked excluded, which is the conservative side).
- **Static copy contents.** `web/dist-proof/`: index, one JS and one CSS asset, `favicon.svg`,
  `data/ledgerline.json`, `data/attacks.json`, `_redirects`, `vercel.json`, `manifest.json` (sha256
  of each file, commit, build time). Grepped the bundle and data for user names, drive paths,
  `near-credentials`, `ed25519:` keys, API keys, `secret`, `anthropic`, hex-64 strings and
  `localhost`/`127.0.0.1`: nothing but the word "password" twice inside the Showcase sample copy. The
  only URLs are `testnet.nearblocks.io`, React's error URL and SVG namespaces; no fonts, RPC or
  wallet code. `SECRETISH` is applied to both data files at build time.
- **Routes in the copy.** `/`, `/proof/ledgerline`, `/attacks`, `/example/ledgerline` (the Try panel
  runs from the bundled sample, no call), `/example/ledgerline?tab=acquire`, and an unknown route
  ("Not part of this copy", link home) all render; no console errors. The nav marks the current page.
- **Screens.** At 1440 the strip is four columns per sale and a shaded "Now" row; at 390 (browser
  pane emulation) the strip, the economics page and the static landing stack with no horizontal
  overflow (`scrollWidth` 390 on each). Earlier headless captures at 390 width clipped text because
  they ran without mobile emulation; those files were removed so they don't mislead.
- **Checks run.** `npm run lint` (2 pre-existing warnings), `npm run typecheck`, the six test files
  for these pieces (22 tests pass), `npm run attacks -- --check` (27/27, 5/5), `npm run proof-site`.
  The full `npm test` was not run.
