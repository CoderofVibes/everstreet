# Everstreet: start your business here

Astra launch-pad direction for Tony and Claude · 2026-11-01  
Proposal for the product, interaction, visual system and NEAR architecture. Claude owns implementation; Tony decides scope and release.

Tony's idea is the center of this pass: **a launch pad for aspiring entrepreneurs, an acquisition marketplace, and a place to find services, all using NEAR.** My earlier pass gave operating continuity too much of the story. Here, the founder's first offer and first customer lead. Operations help the business grow; acquisitions give that growth another possible destination.

All people, demand, prices, businesses and financial histories in the examples are fictional. Dollar figures describe proposed product screens and illustrative calculations, not existing revenue or redeemable test balances. The development product must label fixtures and testnet activity where they appear. Repository findings and assumptions are recorded at the end.

## 1. The product in one page

**Everstreet helps someone start a business, find their first customers, and build something worth owning.** Arrive with an idea, a skill, or simply the wish to work for yourself. Leave the first session with a clear offer, a page you are proud to share, and a practical route to the next person who might need it.

The homepage says **“Start your business here.”** Under it: “Shape your idea. Find your first customers. Build something worth owning.” Its main action is **Start a business**. Beside it are visible entrances for **Find a solution**, **Run my business**, and **Explore acquisitions**. Returning people resume their current work. One account can be a founder, customer and buyer, with a clear acting-context switch.

The heart is a guided launch studio. It helps a founder choose whom to serve, understand the problem, design a first deliverable, set a price and work out how to deliver the first order. AI drafts an editable founder introduction, idea page, offer, storefront and first-week plan. The founder supplies the substance and approves what becomes public. A workshop, local service, physical product and software subscription all belong here.

**Every launch includes a route to customers.** A founder can share a useful page immediately, respond to an opted-in customer request, join a small launch circle, and appear in discovery when the offer meets someone's needs. An idea page can recruit a first tester before there is anything to buy. A storefront can take a booking, trial, order request or purchase when that action is supported. The platform helps turn interest into a specific next action.

As the business grows, the launch studio becomes a daily desk: who is interested, what to deliver today, what to improve next, and what someone else can do. The founder still sees the next achievable growth step. They can add offers, run another class, delegate support, build repeat business and tell the story of what they are learning.

The second pillar is a serious acquisition marketplace. Investors, owner-operators and other businesses create buying profiles and search by category, stage, traction, revenue, acquisition openness and potential value. **A private Value screen grows alongside every operating business.** It turns payment history, delivery records, costs and explicit model assumptions into an understandable valuation range. Founders can explore how their next decisions affect value; buyers can inspect the calculation and compare it with the asking price. A permitted buyer can then examine evidence and accept an exact deal on NEAR. Customer bookings and promises carry through the transfer.

The third pillar is service discovery. “What do you need?” becomes editable constraints and a small set of useful matches: why this, how much, can I use it, and what happens next? These are the businesses launched here. Unmet requests can inspire the next founder, with the requester's permission. Discovery supplies customers; founders supply useful offers; successful businesses attract buyers and new founders.

NEAR makes the growing business more credible and usable: approachable accounts, sponsored transaction fees, payments settled in a supported asset, inspectable receipts, a durable control record, and accepted ownership changes. People experience these as **get paid**, **see where the number comes from**, and **take ownership**. Technical evidence stays one tap away.

The first business model to test is free entry and a useful first storefront, then paid growth tools, team workflows and clearly priced commerce or acquisition services. Organic ranking remains independent of payment. The success measure is founders reaching a fulfilled first order and serving another customer—not the number of plans generated. Everstreet should remain valuable to someone who loves running their business and never wants to sell.

## 2. The founder's journey: from an itch to a growing business

### The product has a beginning, not an empty dashboard

Use three starting choices: **I have an idea**, **Help me find an idea**, and **I've already started**. All lead to a small, editable launch project. No wallet setup, incorporation question or acquisition questionnaire interrupts the first useful draft.

The visible progress is **Shape → Test → Launch → Grow**. It describes what the founder is doing, rather than a score they must complete. A person can publish an idea to test demand before taking money; a founder who can already deliver can go straight to an offer. Pausing or changing direction preserves the work.

The fictional thread through this section is **Mara Solis**, starting **Juniper Print Studio**. She can teach beginner printmaking, has access to a small space on Saturday mornings, and wants a modest business alongside other work. Juniper can use OpenSeat for bookings. Juniper's ticket revenue and OpenSeat's software subscription revenue belong to different businesses and must never be merged.

### When the person has no idea yet

Ask four practical questions, allowing “not sure”:

1. **“What do people already ask you for help with?”** Skills, interests, lived experience, things they make, access to tools or space.
2. **“Who could you reach for an honest conversation?”** A community or customer type, without uploading a contact list.
3. **“What could you give this each week, and what would you feel comfortable spending to try it?”** Time, budget and constraints.
4. **“Would you rather teach, make something, solve a problem for someone, or build a tool?”** More than one is allowed.

Return three **small first-business directions**, not fifty generic startup ideas. Each card contains a customer, a purchasable outcome, a first experiment, a rough effort/cost assumption, and the reason it fits the answers. For Mara:

| Direction | First offer | Smallest experiment | Why this could fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teach a first printmaking session | A beginner leaves with three finished prints | Show a sample and ask about one proposed Saturday session | Uses her teaching skill and existing space |
| Make custom stationery | A small batch designed for one occasion | Offer two sample designs and take a scoped inquiry | Uses her making skill; materials and delivery need checking |
| Help studios present their classes | Rewrite and photograph one class listing | Offer one fixed-scope trial to a studio | Can begin remotely; work samples matter more than equipment |

If consented marketplace demand supports a direction, show the specific relevant request or aggregate with its date. If it does not, say **“Suggested from your answers; demand still to test.”** AI should explain its reasoning without pretending to know whether the business will succeed. A “Show me three different directions” action changes the exploration. Choosing one commits only to an experiment.

**Needs / size:** preference interview, reusable offer patterns, opt-in demand records, editable idea cards and saved alternatives; **M**. Begin with curated patterns and optional AI drafting. No predictive success score is needed.

### The guided interview becomes a live offer

The conversation has a visible output throughout. On a phone, a **Your offer** tab opens the changing card; on desktop it sits beside the interview. Each answer can be edited directly. Ask only the next unresolved question, and offer a plain form throughout.

| Prompt | AI drafts | Founder decides |
|---|---|---|
| “Who is this for?” | A concrete audience and examples of people it does not fit | The people she can serve, including any age or access limits |
| “What do they want to do or change?” | One clear outcome: “Make your first three linocut prints” | Whether this is the actual customer problem |
| “What exactly will they get?” | Deliverable, duration, inclusions, exclusions and customer action | Materials, instruction, capacity and what she can promise |
| “What have you already made or learned?” | A work-sample caption and a short, source-labeled founder story | Which facts and artifacts she can stand behind and publish |
| “What will the first offer cost?” | Two or three price/package experiments with visible cost assumptions | Price, pricing unit, included costs and whether the first trial is free |
| “How will you deliver the first order?” | A fulfillment plan, schedule, preparation tasks and draft cancellation terms | Date, place, supplies, support and the actual remedy policy |

The draft might become: **“Your first linocut print · 90 minutes · materials included · six places · $35 per person.”** If the venue is unconfirmed, the action is **Tell me when it opens**, and the page says the date is proposed. Confirming the venue and delivery plan can later enable a booking. AI never fills a missing credential or venue with plausible copy.

Finish with a compact launch kit: founder profile, idea page, first offer, storefront, three immediate tasks and an invitation draft. Show each as an editable artifact, not a long chat answer. Accepting a suggestion produces a version; rejected copy does not reappear automatically.

**Needs / size:** `LaunchProject`, structured answers, template schema, draft artifacts, versions, factual-claim references and a preview renderer shared with the storefront; **L** for the whole flow, **M** for one template. AI generation is a service behind the form; its outage must not stop saving or publishing a manual offer.

### The first hour

These are experience targets, not a promise of a customer within sixty minutes. The no-idea route may use more time exploring and finish with an idea invitation rather than a bookable offer.

| Time | Screen and action | What the founder leaves with |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 minutes | **Start**: describe an idea or choose a direction | A private project and a sentence naming the intended customer |
| 5–15 | **Shape**: answer the short interview while the offer forms | A specific deliverable she can explain to another person |
| 15–25 | **Try the offer**: inspect price, capacity, delivery and missing facts | A chosen first experiment; unknowns become tasks |
| 25–35 | **Make it yours**: edit profile, sample, idea and storefront | A page that sounds like her and clearly states its stage |
| 35–45 | **Preview as a customer**: use the actual action in a marked preview | Confidence about what a visitor sees and what happens after tapping |
| 45–50 | **Publish**: choose audience and review the exact public fields | A usable page in the selected environment, with a stable link |
| 50–60 | **Find your first people**: choose a shareable invitation, relevant request or launch circle | One distribution action she controls, and a short next-session plan |

Anonymous visitors can draft before signing in. Sign-in saves the project across devices before publication or collecting responses. If they stop midway, the return screen says **“Your workshop is nearly ready. Confirm the location next.”** It does not restart an interview or present empty revenue charts.

The publication review answers five questions in ordinary language: who will see this; what they can do; what is being promised; how the founder will respond; what remains unpublished. “Publish idea” and “Open for bookings” are different actions. A founder may publish an idea before activating NEAR commerce.

### Templates must help deliver a business

Templates include the first customer workflow and its later operation, not only a layout. Support the four business families in the launch editor from the start; enable paid fulfillment as each adapter is ready.

| Template | First useful offer | Fields that matter | Supported first action / later depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class or appointment | A place at a specified time | Capacity, duration, location/remote link, accessibility, materials and cancellation | Interest or a real booking / paid holds, reminders and repeat sessions |
| Scoped service | A clear result by a date | Deliverable, customer inputs, scope, revisions, deadline and remedy | Request a quote or accept a defined scope / milestones and delivery acceptance |
| Physical product | A specific item or small batch | Variants, stock or made-to-order lead time, delivery area, shipping and returns | Product inquiry or launch interest / inventory, paid orders, shipment and returns |
| Software | A tool with a usable trial | Working product link, onboarding, plan, access period, support and cancellation | Try the working tool or request a pilot / subscriptions and bounded renewals |

A physical-product founder gets a credible product page and a materials/shipping checklist immediately. “Payments coming later” must never silently turn an unsupported shipping flow into a buy button. The UI should name what customers can actually do today. Classes and services deserve equal design care to software.

**Needs / size:** common offer schema plus typed template fields; action adapters with `availability`, `request`, `confirm`, `fulfill`, `cancel` and remedy behavior; **M** per basic template, **L** for each complete commerce adapter. An adapter advertises supported actions so the storefront cannot accidentally promise more.

### The first week: make contact, learn, deliver

The desk opens on **This week: get your first useful response**, then advances to **Deliver your first order** when an order exists. Each task has a reason, one action and a small output. The AI adjusts the plan from events and the founder's answers; it does not fill the calendar with generic marketing chores.

| Day / moment | The founder does | Product help and the evidence it creates |
|---|---|---|
| Launch day | Publish a page and choose where to share it | A native share sheet, copyable invitation, printable QR link and a preview card; attribution distinguishes her link from marketplace discovery |
| First responses | Read one useful comment or customer request | Group questions into “unclear offer,” “price,” “timing” and “ready to try”; show original messages before any summary |
| Offer revision | Change one important thing | An editable suggestion such as adding materials to the headline; new terms apply to new orders |
| First booking or accepted scope | Confirm the actual commitment | Customer receipt, schedule, due task and any supported payment; an interest signup remains interest |
| Delivery day | Teach the class, deliver the service or run the trial | A practical preparation checklist, customer input and a clear completion/remedy action |
| After delivery | Ask for specific feedback and permission to quote it | “What worked? What should change?” A review can link to the completed order without publishing the customer's identity |
| Week's end | Choose repeat, revise, pause or try another idea | A short account: what was published, how people found it, what they did, what was learned and one suggested next experiment |

If nobody responds, the product is still useful. It asks whether anyone relevant saw the offer, whether the promise was clear, and whether the founder can reach five suitable people. It can suggest one narrower audience or another time. It cannot invent a demand chart or tell the founder to buy promotion.

**Community at this stage:** opt into a small **First Five** circle with compatible ambitions and availability. Members exchange a specific offer critique or test request. Reciprocity is about useful feedback; nobody has to purchase, endorse or leave a favorable review. The founder can also work privately.

**Needs / size:** inquiry inbox, consent, action attribution, task generation, fulfillment events, approved feedback and small-group moderation; **L** in slices. Sharing and the first-week task list are **S–M** additions to the launch flow. A staffed or curated circle can precede automated matching.

### The first month: turn one sale into a pattern

The top of the desk remains action-oriented: **“Two people asked for an evening session. Open one?”** A small growth strip shows interested people, first customers, repeat customers, paid receipts and earned revenue with definitions. A funnel can be zero without being a failure. The important question is which next action would improve the business.

| Founder opportunity | Screen / next action | What AI helps with | What must be real underneath |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repeat what works | Duplicate an offer into the next date or batch | Draft the next-session description and preparation plan | Fresh capacity, new terms version and customer opt-in |
| Improve conversion | See where suitable visitors stopped; inspect questions | Suggest one clearer promise or an experiment | Attributed events, enough observations, and an “insufficient data” state |
| Reach adjacent customers | Browse relevant published needs and a partner's offers | Draft a specific response or collaboration proposal | Consent, matching constraints and human-approved communication |
| Keep customers | Offer a follow-up class, renewal or related service | Draft an appropriate next offer | Fulfillment history and separate marketing permission |
| Create capacity | Open **Hand this task to someone** | Draft instructions from the task and founder's examples | Named operator, bounded capability, actual result and revocation |
| Understand economics | Open **Value** and **Money** | Explain why receipts, costs and assumptions moved the estimate | Finalized payments, order context, costs and versioned calculation |

The daily desk has three persistent groups: **Customers to help**, **Build the business**, **Hand off a task**. A completed automated promotion belongs in history; a blocked promotion becomes a useful exception. Do not force routine work into the founder's queue merely to make the screen look active.

A first delegation might be **“Let Ellis answer booking questions and manage Saturday's waitlist.”** Show the precise allowed actions and end date. App membership and NEAR operator authority are separate. The founder approves the supported grant; the teammate completes a task without receiving the founder's keys. Customer bookings remain the customers' rights.

**Needs / size:** growth projections, repeat-offer workflow, versioned tasks/runbooks, membership and scoped operator adapter; **L**. Begin with one useful delegation. General autonomous agents and a full accounting suite are unnecessary to make the first month better.

### The first year: a business with choices

The launch project becomes a history of decisions and work: the first offer, customer feedback, changed prices, repeat sales, additional offers, new operators and source-linked financial periods. The founder can tell a credible story because these happened as part of serving customers.

At a year, offer four equally legitimate next chapters:

| Path | What Everstreet makes possible | Next useful screen |
|---|---|---|
| Stay small and profitable | Better offers, repeat customers and time saved | Capacity and earnings plan for the next quarter |
| Grow a team or second location | Repeatable templates, delegated work and separate responsibilities | Duplicate an operating pattern with fresh costs and capacity |
| Buy a complementary business | Use the same account as an acquirer; find an existing customer base or capability | A buying mandate informed by the founder's chosen goals |
| Explore a sale | Privately inspect value, choose buyer visibility and share evidence selectively | Acquisition profile and introductions, with the operating business continuing |

The business should not age out of the launch pad. A new offer, product line or location can run a new launch project inside the same workspace. An acquisition can also be someone's first entrepreneurial step: **“Start by buying something already working.”** Keep that route available without displacing creation as the default.

**Needs / size:** multiple launch projects per business, versioned history, team workflows and separate acquisition context; **M** for the navigation and data relationship, **L–XL** for later portfolio operations. Exportable work and records help a founder keep their options.

## 3. Give founders and ideas a place to be seen

### Four connected public surfaces

The public product should have people and possibility in it. A new founder belongs here before revenue. Give them something specific to show and a useful invitation to make.

| Surface | What visitors see and can do | Why it matters / what it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder profile** | “Mara teaches people to make their first print.” Relevant experience, work samples, projects, what she wants help with, and optional availability. Follow a project or send an allowed inquiry | Gives the person credibility before a business has a history. Profile, artifact permissions, project links and contact preferences; **M** |
| **Idea page** | Intended customer, problem, what exists today, next experiment, proposed offer and one clear ask: “Would you try this?” or “Help choose the first date” | Makes an unfinished idea discussable. Versioned idea, stage, experiment, response form and consent; **M** |
| **Business page / storefront** | What the business does, active offers, useful samples, founder story, support and selected milestones. Book, try, request or buy according to support | Converts discovery into action. Stable business record, offer versions and action adapters; **L** |
| **Update** | One change with an artifact and a next action: “We added an evening class after these requests. Six places open.” | Gives people a reason to return and a founder a reason to publish. Update, referenced offer or experiment, consented evidence and follow preferences; **S–M** |

An idea can become a business without losing the people who followed it. Preserve the idea URL and its history, link it to the storefront, and let followers choose launch notifications. Interest in an idea never silently becomes an order or a subscription. A founder may have several ideas; each can remain private, pause or close with a short explanation.

For Juniper, the first idea page could read:

> **Make something with your Saturday.**  
> Mara is testing a beginner printmaking workshop in the fictional town of Alder Bay.  
> Available to see: a sample print and a 60-second process walkthrough.  
> Proposed first session: 90 minutes, materials included, $35 per person. Venue confirmation pending.  
> Help shape it: Saturday morning or an evening?  
> **Tell me which time works**

That is enough to begin. A founder does not need a fictional testimonial, an invented revenue target or a glossy pitch deck to earn attention.

### The showcase: a street of things starting

Create a public **Explore** surface with four views: **Starting now**, **Open for customers**, **What people need**, and **Following**. This is where founders advertise their work and visitors discover what is emerging. Its cards carry one meaningful action. The homepage can feature a short selection; the full surface supports category, location/remote and interest filters.

**Starting now** includes published ideas seeking feedback and launches seeking their first customers. Clearly separate those states. **Open for customers** contains usable offers. **What people need** contains requests people deliberately published. **Following** is chronological and controllable. Acquisition listings live in their own entrance.

Give new offers a fair chance among relevant, eligible results. As an initial experiment, reserve one of the first five showcase positions for a suitable recent launch when one exists. Label it **New here**; do not insert it into a strict service search unless it meets the search constraints. Measure useful responses and fulfilled outcomes, not applause. This is a product-policy experiment, not a permanent ranking formula.

Start with finite collections such as **This week's first offers**, **Useful for independent studios**, and **Made near you**. Let a visitor finish browsing. A public like count is not the organizing principle. Good evidence includes a sample, a clear deliverable, a responsive founder and real availability; early creators can have all four.

**Needs / size:** distinct public projections for ideas and live offers, editorial collections, follow subscriptions, eligibility, freshness, reporting and moderation; **L**. A small curated shelf plus following is enough to start. Personalized infinite scroll is a poor first use of build time.

### A distribution kit at every launch

After publication, open **Find your first customers** with three routes:

| Route | Founder action | Product behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Bring your people** | Copy or share a specific invitation; download a QR card; add a link to an existing profile | Preview exact copy and destination. Record the share channel only where available; unknown traffic remains unknown. No contact scraping or automatic posting |
| **Meet existing demand** | Read a consented request, decide whether the offer fits, draft a response | Explain the match, preserve the requester's conditions and give the founder an explicit Send action in the future product |
| **Be found here** | Choose relevant categories, service area, audience and availability; join a launch collection | Show where the offer is eligible and why it is absent from another collection. Never sell a better organic position |

AI can produce a short personal invitation, a community post, a business introduction and a product-description variant. It can highlight the line that changes for each audience. The founder controls tone, recipients and publication. A change of price in the workspace should flag older share drafts for review rather than silently rewriting an already shared commitment.

**Needs / size:** versioned share drafts, channel/referral identifiers, link previews, native sharing, simple QR output and attribution; **M**. This is valuable before Everstreet has marketplace traffic. Do not claim internal distribution that the platform has not yet earned.

### Credibility before revenue, and after it

Use four consistent source labels on individual claims: **Verified on NEAR**, **Connected**, **Founder supplied**, and **Estimate**. Derived claims can show mixed inputs. An estimate does not inherit a verified label because one input is a transaction. Add an independent environment/origin label such as **Fictional example**, **Seeded history**, **Sandbox**, or **Live testnet**. “Verified on NEAR · Testnet” can truthfully describe a transaction with no commercial value.

| Stage | Credibility worth showing | What the label means |
|---|---|---|
| Exploring | Work sample, relevant experience, thoughtful experiment | The sample exists; experience is founder supplied unless separately supported |
| Testing | Unique consented expressions of interest, test participation, dated learning | Interest and test use, with period and duplicate handling; neither is revenue |
| First customers | A completed order, service-linked feedback, response time | A defined event occurred; a review's independence is a separate question |
| Growing | Repeat orders, delivered outcomes, net receipts, revenue periods and costs | Reproducible definitions and source coverage, with missing data visible |
| Available to acquire | Selected economics, estimate, asking price, transfer scope | Seller-approved acquisition information; no blanket company certification |

An authenticated account or wallet is not proof of a distinct independent customer. Exclude declared owner/team transactions from public customer traction, flag related activity and suspicious circular flows, and show unresolved qualifications in financial evidence. A review cannot be bought into a stronger source badge.

Visibility controls read **Only me/team**, **Invited people**, **Public**, and **Selected buyer room**. Previews use the actual server projections. Raw interviews, customer details, private costs and acquisition intent stay out of public search and general AI retrieval. Moderation needs reporting, blocking, removal and correction from the first shared cohort. Published-chain transaction details remain public regardless of application settings.

**Needs / size:** claim definitions, source links, consent, audience projections and abuse handling; **M–L**. These support advertising without turning founders into performers chasing an invented success score.

## 4. Acquisitions and a valuation feature worth opening

### A buying account with a clear purpose

The second pillar has its own substantial product. An individual buyer, small investment team or strategic business creates a buying workspace under the same personal account. Choose **Operate it myself**, **Add it to my business**, or **Acquire with a team**. An investor can research and pursue acquisitions here; a separate fundraising or fractional-investment product is not assumed.

Create a **buying brief**: the problem the buyer wants to solve, categories, geography/remote requirements, stage, budget and acceptable ongoing workload. Add collaborators with research, evidence and approval roles. A team membership is not automatically the account authorized to accept a sale.

Northline Studio Tools might save: “Software or services for independent studios; open to acquisition; repeat customers; remote delivery; up to $150,000 asking price; enough information to assess owner workload.” The buyer can then add a potential-value range, revenue period, source coverage or growth constraint. Natural language edits the same visible filters as the form.

| Step | What the acquirer sees and does | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Set criteria | Buying brief with hard requirements and preferences | A saved mandate and useful empty-result behavior |
| Search | Opted-in businesses with customer proposition, stage, availability, selected traction, asking price and estimated range | A shortlist whose reasons can be inspected |
| Compare | Three businesses across the same period, currency, valuation basis and disclosed scope | Differences are understandable; unknown figures remain unknown |
| Ask for access | Request specific evidence, name the buying team, state purpose and duration | A founder can accept, narrow or decline the request |
| Understand | Financials, aggregate customer behavior, costs, operator workload, obligations and transfer scope | The buyer reproduces a material metric and adjusts a private scenario |
| Agree | Scope, price, evidence snapshot, conditions, expiry and exact accepted version | Both parties know what will change on chain and what remains to do |
| Take over | Supported control transfer, proceeds state and first-shift tasks | The buyer appoints operators and serves an existing customer |

**Needs / size:** organization/membership, mandate, seller projection, saved search, compare, grants, rooms and existing sale adapter; **L** for discovery and rooms, **L** more for a complete phone closing flow.

### Make valuation a headline feature, early

The product promise is **“See what you are building.”** Value is a first-class founder tab and a first-class acquisition-search criterion. It should arrive soon after supported payments and basic cost entry, not after a full accounting product or twelve months of waiting.

The founder sees a range, the time and scope it describes, the inputs that move it, and **Try a change**. The buyer sees the seller-shared range, its calculation and a separate asking price. Both can trace financial inputs to records. The range remains an **Estimate**. This is a useful model of potential business value, not a certificate from the blockchain.

NEAR gives this feature a strong foundation: the same system taking an order records the supported settlement and refund. We can observe money movement without asking a founder to retype every sale. Order and delivery records explain what the money was for; cost records and assumptions explain earnings; the model explains value. **Known payment activity becomes usable financial evidence, then a transparent estimate.**

Use four stages of the same Value screen:

| Business stage | What is shown now | How it becomes more informative |
|---|---|---|
| Idea, no customers | **Build a value plan**: “At this price, volume, delivery cost and workload, what could this become?” Entirely a founder scenario | Save the target; compare actual first orders with the assumptions. Do not put a price tag on the idea itself |
| First paid period | **Early estimate · short history** based on the completed period and explicit annualization/cost assumptions | Show the observed period prominently and allow seasonality or capacity adjustments. Never label one month's annualization trailing annual earnings |
| Several operating periods | **Operating estimate** using rolling results, refunds, repeat behavior and normalized costs | Compare conservative, current-pattern and growth cases; distinguish observed and forecast inputs |
| Mature operating history | **Operating estimate · trailing basis**, with a comparable-informed model where suitable evidence exists | Improve category calibration and disclose comparable cohort, date, scope and sample size |

If a material cost is unknown, the founder can enter a visible cost assumption and still explore a **Scenario**. The published estimate names that assumption; it cannot call the result observed profit. A business that is loss-making may use an explicit asset or future-cash-flow scenario appropriate to its scope. The screen remains useful without forcing every business into a positive multiple.

### What forms the number

Start with an explainable earnings model for small operating businesses. Separate an optional recurring-revenue model for software and an asset model for businesses where equipment/inventory matters. A workshop, consultancy and subscription tool must not share an unexplained universal multiple.

The first model can implement:

~~~text
Normalized annual operating earnings
  = annualized or trailing earned revenue
  - recurring operating costs
  - cost of the founder's work that a successor must replace
  +/- individually explained normalization adjustments

Estimated operating value range
  = normalized earnings x stated low/high earnings multiples

Illustrative transaction consideration
  = operating value
  + explicitly included excess cash / separately valued assets
  - explicitly assumed debt / separately treated liabilities
~~~

Record what the operating value already includes. Inventory, customer reserves and working capital are not casually added or subtracted twice. A managed-rights package with no transferable assets cannot inherit the value of an entire legal company. The model version contains the scope and adjustment rules.

For software, the founder may compare a recurring-revenue scenario with an earnings scenario. Recurring revenue must derive from actual plans, active entitlements and billing periods, with canceled, free and unpaid accounts handled explicitly. Show the chosen revenue multiple, retention and margin assumptions; do not present two models as two independently verified sale prices.

**Where multiples come from:** the first development model uses explicitly declared illustrative assumptions and is labeled **Scenario model**. It is immediately usable for testing growth decisions and the acquisition UI. A production model should version category assumptions and incorporate appropriately licensed or consented completed-transaction evidence as it becomes available. Asking prices can be compared with an estimate; they cannot masquerade as closed-sale comparables. Do not invent market statistics to make the range look authoritative.

**Potential value is searchable from the first usable model.** The filter chooses **Scenario / Operating estimate / Comparable-informed estimate** and the visible basis. Include unestimated candidates by default as a separate group. A range filter matches overlapping published ranges, not an undisclosed hidden estimate. An asking-price budget is a separate constraint and the clearest affordability filter. An anonymous seller is filtered only on the bands they deliberately disclose.

### A worked example Claude can reproduce

**Fictional future OpenSeat scenario, in illustrative USD; not the gate-1 fixture and not a market appraisal.** The software business has now operated for three complete months in this hypothetical example. All quarterly earned revenue is assumed to have corresponding NEAR-settled order receipts; costs come from supplied records and labor is estimated. The displayed multiple assumptions are arbitrary inputs for this example.

| Input | Amount | Basis / source label in the example |
|---|---:|---|
| Earned revenue over the latest three complete months | $30,000 | Order/service-period ledger derived from finalized receipt evidence; three-month window |
| Annualized revenue | $120,000 | Estimate: $30,000 × 4; flat continuation, not a forecast of guaranteed sales |
| Recurring operating costs for those three months | $16,500 | Founder supplied cost records, assumed complete for the example |
| Annualized recurring costs | $66,000 | Estimate: $16,500 × 4 |
| Annual replacement labor | $18,000 | Estimate entered for this model |
| Normalized annual earnings | $36,000 | $120,000 − $66,000 − $18,000 |
| Low / high earnings multiples | 2.0× / 3.0× | Illustrative model assumptions, not observed market multiples |
| **Potential operating value** | **$72,000–$108,000** | **Estimate · Scenario model · three months annualized** |
| Separate founder asking price | $95,000 | Founder supplied; zero cash/debt/other adjustments assumed in this example |

The first screen can be bold and readable:

> **$72k–$108k potential operating value**  
> Estimate · Scenario model · 3 months annualized  
> Based on $36k normalized annual earnings and 2–3× assumptions.  
> Receipts traced to NEAR; costs supplied; replacement labor estimated.  
> **See the calculation** · **Try a change**

Those source labels describe the proposed functioning feature. In the fictional development fixture, **Seeded history** also appears and opens the fixture source; it is never relabeled observed production revenue.

Now let the founder ask **“What if we add five $20 monthly customers?”** With a stated $5 monthly variable cost each, no extra replacement labor, twelve full paid service months and no churn, the scenario adds $900 annual earnings: `5 × ($20 − $5) × 12`. The range becomes **$73,800–$110,700** at the same multiples. It is a saved “what if,” not an immediate revision to observed value. Actual completed periods can later replace those assumptions.

Another scenario adds $6,000 of necessary replacement labor. Annual earnings fall to $30,000 and the range becomes **$60,000–$90,000**. The interface can explain why documentation and delegation matter without hiding labor costs or promising that every delegated task raises value.

### A number that changes for understandable reasons

Recompute receipt metrics after finalized outcomes and corrections. Update the estimate when its defined period/input set changes, normally in a daily materialized snapshot; show last source date and last calculation date separately. A chart marks **first paid order**, **repeat customer**, **new offer**, **cost correction** and **model change**. Model changes are distinguishable from business progress.

**What changed?** separates revenue, costs, replacement labor, forecast assumptions, multiple and transaction-scope adjustments. It should account for the movement rather than give a generic “growth improved” explanation. A new customer has a different effect on current recurring run rate and on earned revenue already delivered.

Show two short evidence measures next to the detail: **Observed period** and **Source coverage**. For example, “3 complete months; 100% of declared customer receipt amount traced to supported NEAR payments; costs supplied.” The denominator is declared receipts, not an assertion that hidden off-platform activity cannot exist. Show unresolved source gaps directly. Never collapse evidence strength and prediction uncertainty into a made-up confidence percentage.

A founder can keep the range private, share a coarse band, or share a precise version with a buyer. A buyer can save their own labor, margin or synergy assumptions without modifying the founder's estimate. Accepted deal evidence stays frozen at its agreed version; subsequent financial changes are disclosed through a new version and reviewed when material.

**Needs / size:** order-aware receipt projection, lightweight cost records, metric definitions, versioned model inputs, reproducible calculator, source drawer, scenario saves and disclosure projections; **L** for a useful first Value product. A commercially calibrated comparable model is later **XL** work. The first calculator, search field and founder growth feedback do not wait for that later model.

### From search to a deal

Founder acquisition settings are **Not considering**, **Open to introductions**, or **Listed for acquisition**. Within the last two, choose a named listing, approved anonymous teaser or invitation-only access. None of these settings changes customer ranking or access to launch tools. A business may keep growing while buyers explore it.

The first evidence request can be small: “Northline requests 90-day aggregate revenue, cost assumptions and operator workload for seven days.” The founder previews exactly those fields. A later grant can add customer cohorts, obligations or private documents. Grants bind team, business, categories, purpose and expiry; all reads and exports enforce them. Revocation ends future application access, with downloaded copies and public-chain data outside that promise.

The room begins with **Why this business**, **How it earns**, **What you would run**, and **What you would acquire**. Lead with the product and economics, then show relevant customer commitments. A diligence assistant answers from allowed records and keeps source labels. Buyer messages cannot instruct it to retrieve another business's private data.

Use the existing sale foundation: a committed evidence/scope packet, two parties accepting the same digest, buyer funding held for those terms, owner change and generation increment, and retained customer reserves. The UI distinguishes **control transferred**, **seller proceeds available**, and **external transfer items complete**. Revised material terms require fresh acceptances. The buyer's first shift includes appointing a worker and honoring a pre-sale customer action. That is where continuity becomes a powerful acquisition feature.

## 5. Service discovery brings the first customers

### From a need to an action

The service entrance opens on **“What do you need?”** with examples for an individual, a business and a physical product. Anyone can browse. A useful answer is a small set of offers that can meet the need, each with a definite next action.

For **“A beginner printmaking class this Saturday, under $40, materials included”**, show editable chips for outcome, date/timezone, budget/unit, location, experience and materials. Mark hard requirements separately from preferences. If location is missing and changes the result, ask that one question. Keyword search, category browsing and the form remain available without AI.

Each match answers four questions:

| Question | Juniper card in the fictional design example |
|---|---|
| **Why this?** | Beginner linocut; materials included; selected Saturday and area |
| **How much?** | $35 per person, with the total and any unavoidable fees shown before confirmation |
| **Can I use it?** | Two seats shown by the booking service, checked at a stated time; access information stated or unconfirmed |
| **What happens next?** | “Choose a seat” opens the date, terms and actual booking action |

Hard constraints filter first. Unknown required accessibility or delivery facts mean the offer is not a confirmed match; a separate **Ask to confirm** group is optional. Ranking can use outcome fit, confirmed availability, relevant delivery evidence and responsiveness. Founder subscription tier, acquisition status, valuation and platform fees cannot affect organic order. Suitable new founders receive exploration opportunities without being represented as experienced providers.

The customer completes the supported action and sees a receipt in **My activity**. A waitlist place says waitlist. A quote request says when to expect a response. A confirmed order names the deliverable, price, date, support and remedy route. The customer can return through the same reference if the business changes hands.

### When there is no match, create an opportunity with consent

For **“A left-handed carving kit delivered tomorrow”**, if stock or delivery cannot be established, explain which condition blocked a match. Offer an intentional relaxation, or **Share this request**. The requester previews a redacted need, chooses audience/expiry and decides whether to allow provider replies. Contact details stay private.

This creates a launch loop:

~~~text
A customer publishes a need
        |
A suitable founder sees the request and its constraints
        |
The founder drafts a small offer or a nonfinancial pilot invitation
        |
The requester chooses whether to hear more
        |
A published offer becomes available; a new, explicit order is accepted
        |
Delivery and payment improve the offer's evidence and the founder's next launch
~~~

An existing request is more useful than “people might want this,” but it is not a promised sale. Expire stale needs, limit irrelevant responses and let the requester close the request. Do not disclose private searches as founder leads. Aggregate demand views need explicit permitted use and suppression of revealing small groups; early versions can rely entirely on deliberately published requests.

### The marketplace starts with a useful neighborhood

Keep the platform capable of classes, services, products and software. Give the first catalog depth around independent creative businesses and their customers: a Juniper workshop, an OpenSeat tool, a listing-photography service, a product-page service and a materials-kit inquiry. That produces both B2C and B2B discovery without making “only SaaS” the product identity.

Cold start has a practical answer: founders bring their own shareable offers; curated collections connect relevant offers; consented needs guide new supply. A founder gets a useful storefront and workflow before internal discovery has traffic. Future merchant referrals can connect complementary offers, with customer consent and clear responsibility for each purchase.

Measure time to a useful response, eligible matches, completed customer actions, first-time founder customers, repeat customers and unfulfilled requests. Distinguish the founder's own links, marketplace discovery, referrals and unknown attribution. A search click alone is not a first customer.

**Needs / size:** public offer index, typed constraints, availability adapters, deterministic filtering, source-backed explanations, request consent and customer activity; **L**. Launch with one completed booking path plus honest inquiries for adjacent types. Natural-language interpretation is an enhancement over working filters.

## 6. NEAR underneath: the business really works

### Give each component a clear job

The launch studio is fast, editable and mostly off chain. Commerce and ownership have real NEAR authority. These layers meet through versioned orders, commitments and confirmed events.

~~~text
Founder, customer or buyer on a phone
  |
  +-- Launch / showcase / finder / daily desk / Value / acquisition UI
  |       |
  |       +-- Platform API + database
  |       |     people, ideas, offers, inquiries, teams, grants, drafts
  |       |
  |       +-- Operating adapters
  |       |     OpenSeat bookings; later service, software and goods flows
  |       |
  |       +-- Metrics and valuation service
  |             source records -> classifications -> metrics -> estimates
  |
  +-- User-approved signing + sponsored relay
          |
          +-- Business factory -> one locked contract per business
          |                         owner, operators, money, reserves, sale
          |
          +-- Accepted NEAR token contract
                    |
            finalized outcomes -> indexer -> order and financial projections
~~~

The app controls content publication and scoped evidence access. The business contract controls its owner and registered funds. The operating service controls capacity and delivery state. The valuation service computes an interpretation from these sources. A receipt can be independently checked while an off-chain delivery assertion still relies on the service or its evidence.

Start with the brief's TypeScript app/server, relational database and durable worker, using Claude's familiar choices. Keep OpenSeat independently runnable behind an adapter. A separate infrastructure program, generic agent platform or microservice fleet is unnecessary for the first launch experience.

### One account, with NEAR introduced when it helps

**Recommended experience:** ordinary sign-in using a passkey or supported email/social login; no seed phrase or browser extension as the default path. Activate a NEAR controlling account when the founder enables payments or a buyer prepares an ownership action. The founder sees **“Set up payments and business control”**, the account/recovery choice, what becomes public and who can authorize actions.

NEAR Auth is a concrete integration candidate: its official documentation describes familiar logins backed by MPC signing, testnet development and approval requirements for mainnet credentials. Treat it as a dependency to validate on Tony's phone, including recovery and signing permissions; a browser passkey by itself is not the NEAR transaction signer. Keep a user-controlled external-wallet route available. [NEAR Auth documentation](https://docs.near.org/web3-apps/tutorials/near-auth)

There are two different accounts: the person's controlling account and the business's locked contract account. The person can recover or change the former under the chosen recovery policy; nobody silently adds an upgrade key to the latter. The platform must not retain unilateral full control of every founder and buyer account under a friendly login screen. A seller's recovered personal account does not regain a sold business.

Sponsor account initialization, supported transaction fees and required token storage within a declared budget. NEAR meta transactions allow a user-signed delegated action to be relayed, with the relayer funding execution and attached deposits. The relay must enforce allowed destinations/actions, operation limits and replay/expiry rules. Sponsorship removes a gas-management task from the user; it does not supply customer purchase funds. [NEAR meta transactions](https://docs.near.org/protocol/transactions/meta-tx)

For testnet, a clearly labeled faucet action can fund the customer's supported test balance. The production checkout target is a familiar payment choice with an exact total: an integrated card/on-ramp partner delivers the accepted asset to the supported order flow, or an existing holder pays with that asset. Selecting and validating that partner is new work; only implemented payment methods appear. Keep the partner charge/conversion, NEAR settlement and any card refund/chargeback as distinct states. Account abstraction alone does not make a card purchase settle on NEAR.

Show a human-readable approval for money, powerful permissions and ownership. Routine permitted service work can use narrow authority. Ordinary NEAR function-call keys cannot attach NEAR; the existing contract's one-yocto privileged methods therefore need a deposit-capable signing path. Method restrictions alone also do not encode spending limits or service-resource limits. [NEAR access keys](https://docs.near.org/protocol/accounts-contracts/access-keys), [one-yocto method protection](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/security/one_yocto)

**Needs / size:** app authentication, signing adapter, account provisioning, relay limits, operation persistence, token-storage setup and a tested recovery route; **L**. If the default signing integration is unavailable, preserve the launch product and clearly expose the supported wallet alternative for chain actions. Never silently replace independent approval with a platform-held universal signer.

Names below are brand-neutral. Existing names identify source APIs; proposed names describe the next design, not deployed methods.

| **Proposed business v2: orders** | `register_order(order_id, payer, asset, amount, terms_hash, expiry, reserve_policy)`, `ft_on_transfer(sender_id, amount, msg)`, `get_order`, `get_operation` | Authorized checkout operator registers exact terms; FT receiver accepts only the allowed asset and matching valid order, or declared funding purpose. New operator grants should express capability, applicable resources, expiry and limits, all bound to the authority generation. Initial checkout authorization can be an explicit on-chain order registration rather than a bespoke signed-quote verifier. That costs an extra operation but makes the first payment design inspectable. Optimize only after the supported flow works.

For settlement, the buyer pre-funds the accepted token into a claim held by that business contract. The final matching acceptance can move internal claims, change owner and increment generation in one execution. Paying tokens out of the contract is a later asynchronous withdrawal. NEAR cross-contract calls are independent; a failed external call does not globally undo a prior successful state change. [NEAR cross-contract execution](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/anatomy/crosscontract)

Keep the next deployment born locked. The current v1 has no upgrade or migration method. New capabilities require a new account; old obligations remain at their existing authority until properly resolved. A stable platform business ID can link deployment history but cannot move old balances by renaming a pointer. Build bounded/paginated history and lazy recovery of obsolete holds; the current close loops through operators and other open deals and should not become the unbounded design for years of commerce.

Consequently, phase 3 starts new paid activity in a new deployment; it does not upgrade the existing OpenSeat account. Later contract features, including automated mandates if omitted from v2, first ship in a new version for new businesses. Existing businesses can keep supported manual renewals. Moving an active business between versions requires a separately designed adoption/migration flow. The first atomic acquisition covers one active control/payment contract; it cannot claim to atomically sell several legacy contracts merely because the UI groups them together.

| Record | On NEAR | Off chain |
|---|---|---|
| Founder / idea | Nothing required to draft or publish | Profile, skills, samples, idea, interview, experiment and consent |
| Business | Contract account, owner, generation, supported asset/configuration | Stable platform ID, brand, storefront, templates and deployment lineage |
| Offer / order | Opaque order ID, payment-relevant terms commitment, payer/account linkage, amount, expiry and reserve rule where enforced | Description, customer's identity/contact, price presentation, tax/shipping detail, accepted order snapshot |
| Booking / delivery | Supported payment/reserve outcome; optional receipt commitment | Capacity, attendees, waitlist, location details, delivery and customer remedy evidence |
| Money / metrics | Actual supported deposits, holds, payouts, refunds and their events | Commercial classification, earned-period allocation, cost records, calculations and coverage |
| Value | Optional snapshot commitment, not an on-chain oracle price | Model version, assumptions, low/high results, scenarios and selected disclosure |
| Acquisition | Parties, exact accepted digest, funding state, control transition and generation | Private evidence, negotiations, transfer manifest and external completion records |

Never put booking bearer references, attendee data, raw customer needs, contracts or evidence-room documents in public arguments or event logs. Use opaque identifiers and salted commitments where appropriate. Public accounts, amounts and timing can still be correlated; a private buyer room controls documents, not visibility of public settlement. The app must explain that once at activation and where disclosure choices are made.

### A complete paid-order flow

The first commerce asset should be one explicitly identified test FT on NEAR. Use its contract identity, network and decimal metadata, not just a ticker. Production can later choose a supported stable-value settlement asset; existing DEMO credits never convert into redeemable money.

1. **Prepare.** The service validates the offer version and creates a short-lived capacity hold or accepted scope. An authorized checkout operator registers the matching order commitment, amount, expiry, payer and reserve policy on the new business contract.
2. **Approve.** Checkout shows the total, deliverable and remedy. The customer approves the exact supported transfer. The app persists the operation before submission and resumes it after refresh.
3. **Receive.** `ft_transfer_call` invokes the business's `ft_on_transfer`. Validate the FT predecessor, original sender, exact amount and registered purpose. Invalid or expired transfers return unused funds through the token protocol; they do not create sales. [NEAR FT receiver and refund guidance](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/security/cross-contract-calls/ft-transfer-call)
4. **Confirm.** The indexer checks successful finalized receipt outcomes, including relevant downstream resolution. Inclusion alone is insufficient. Deduplicate the observation, record the accepted amount and confirm the service action idempotently. [NEAR execution and finality guidance](https://docs.near.org/integrations/blocks-and-finality)
5. **Serve.** The customer sees the booking/order and stable reference. The founder sees the next delivery task. Earned revenue follows the declared delivery or service-period rule; it is not automatically identical to cash received.
6. **Resolve.** A late payment with an expired capacity hold produces **Payment received; seat unavailable** and the supported refund path. A service outage produces **Payment received; confirmation pending**. The worker reconciles before retrying; the user is not asked to pay again because a response timed out.
7. **Refund or release.** Apply the original terms and record the outcome. Keep outgoing transfers pending until confirmed; private callbacks bind recipient, asset, amount and operation. A failed payout restores only the appropriate claim. Owner changes must not redirect an already authorized payment.

The paid-capacity hold, customer refund path and token ledger are new work. The existing OpenSeat booking path does not implement them. Begin with one delivery type and a simple, explicit cancellation rule. An owner saying “delivered” or an AI classifier is not enough to release every protected reserve. Some products need customer acknowledgment, time windows or a named dispute process.

For software, one paid access period is enough to start. Add renewals with prepaid, capped mandates later. A scheduled worker is required; contracts do not charge a subscription by waking themselves. Changing owner cannot enlarge the customer's original authorization.

### From NEAR receipts to numbers people can trust

Build the financial path from the order outward:

~~~text
Allowed contract + successful finalized payment/refund
  -> immutable source observation, uniquely keyed by network/receipt/event
  -> matching order, accepted terms and service-period/delivery record
  -> classification and correction history
  -> receipts, earned revenue, costs, repeat behavior and source coverage
  -> versioned valuation inputs
  -> an Estimate with a reproducible calculation
~~~

Customer payment, owner capital, buyer acquisition funding, withdrawal, refund and a transfer between the business's own accounts must have different purposes. Count an order once, even if the token transfer, receiving-contract log and connector import all describe it. Exclude seeded activity, test payments and related-party activity from any eventual production traction series. Keep separate environments and ledgers.

Define a compact metric set first:

| Metric | Definition the product must preserve |
|---|---|
| Net customer receipts | Accepted customer payments minus completed customer refunds for the stated period/asset; exclude capital and acquisition funds |
| Earned revenue | Amount assigned to delivered outcomes or elapsed service periods under the displayed recognition policy; separate prepaid undelivered amounts |
| Current recurring run rate | Active qualifying recurring plans at their current rate; not proof of past annual revenue |
| Repeat customers | Distinct application customer accounts with qualifying repeat paid/fulfilled actions in a stated cohort/window, after declared exclusions; independence can remain unproven |
| Operating earnings input | Earned revenue minus stated recurring costs and normalization items; missing costs remain unknown or explicitly assumed |
| Available funds | The contract's amount available after registered reserves and pending operations; neither revenue nor business value |

Corrections create new entries/snapshots rather than erasing accepted historical evidence. Keep amounts as integer base units with asset metadata. If showing a converted reporting currency, retain the original asset amount and dated rate; token-price changes should not appear as customer growth. Payment-source coverage, cost coverage and customer independence are separate questions.

The source drawer explains the definition, period, original records, calculation, coverage and freshness. Buyer exports retain those labels. This is enough to make a useful early estimate inspectable without first building tax accounting or reconciling every possible bank connector.

### Data and AI boundaries Claude can build from

Start with `Person`, `Organization`, `Membership`, `Business`, `LaunchProject`, `IdeaVersion`, `OfferVersion`, `PublishedNeed`, `Interest`, `Inquiry`, `Order`, `Task`, `Consent` and `Update`. Add `Deployment`, `ChainOperation`, `PaymentObservation`, `MetricSnapshot`, `CostRecord`, `ValuationSnapshot`, `BuyerMandate`, `AccessGrant` and `DealVersion` as the corresponding phase ships. Use brand-neutral table/event identifiers, tenant-scoped IDs and a transactional outbox.

Public storefront, public ideas, acquisition teasers and granted evidence need separate field-whitelisted projections. Search snippets, counts, notification previews and AI retrieval use the same boundaries as pages. The model may draft, interpret a need, explain a calculation and suggest a next action. Publication, disclosure, messages, authority changes and money effects remain explicit user actions through typed authorized tools.

Bind a connected signing identity to the authenticated person with a verified, scoped account-ownership challenge. After chain activation, privileged business administration must follow the current on-chain owner/generation, not a permanent app “founder” flag. A sale invalidates old administrative sessions and worker authority; the buyer's independently verified account gains the relevant workspace control. Personal profiles and customers' own orders remain separate rights. Platform publication/moderation powers do not become a way to change the contract owner.

Before connecting more operator actions, tighten the existing command path: include explicit network/service domain in the next envelope; enforce relevant key permissions and capability scope; recheck operation deduplication with the effect in the same database transaction. Use a handoff barrier and reconcile in-flight work around a sale. A final-state authority read is not a lock over a later off-chain mutation. These are focused engineering follow-ups to the inspected source, not work performed in this pass.

### Before real money: one short decision list

Tony must approve the production scope and public launch. ## 8. Wild launch-pad ideas

**★ marks an idea I would bet on.** These are extensions, not a request to put all of them in the first release. Each starts with something a founder or prospective customer can do.

|---|---|---|

## 9. Build order: each phase ends on Tony's phone

Build product slices with real persistence, authorization and working actions in a private development environment. Fictional data and testnet are development boundaries, not a script the user has to follow. Tony should choose his own fictional idea, edit it, leave, return and try the result from a second session.

Use a local phone-reachable preview or a separately authorized private deployment. Nothing in this pass authorizes public hosting, posting, mainnet, spending or submission. Day One keeps its stated priority; these are useful stopping points, not a new deadline commitment.

Sizes throughout this pass are relative: **S** a contained surface/change; **M** a short end-to-end workflow; **L** a substantial product slice or integration; **XL** a major subsystem that should be split further. They are not calendar estimates. Use the following order; implementation details remain Claude's.

### Phase 1 — Put the launch pad itself in his hand

**Ships:** the launch-first homepage and four entrances; account/context foundation; “have an idea / find an idea / already started”; guided interview; founder profile; editable idea and offer; storefront preview; audience-controlled publication; a working interest/inquiry action; first-week checklist. Offer templates cover all four families, initially exposing only supported inquiry/interest actions. One good class or service template gets the deepest guidance.

**Underneath:** app shell, persistent launch records, typed templates, versions, session authorization, public/private projections, response storage and shared preview renderer. Use curated suggestions and manual editing if AI is not ready; add real AI drafting behind the same contract. No blockchain prerequisite for drafting and publication.

**Tony's phone finish:** start with “I know printmaking, but I don't know what to sell”; choose a direction; change the price and deliverable; publish the page in the private preview; open its link in a separate visitor session; send a fictional inquiry; return to find that inquiry and his next task. Refresh and resume without losing anything. Also take the direct “I have an idea” route.

**Done when:** the founder can produce their own page, visitor response and follow-up task without a prewritten actor script; private drafts stay private; AI absence does not break the form; a preview cannot accidentally create an order; published content has a clear supported action. The other entrances are visible but only expose functionality that works, with honest empty states.

**Size / cut:** **L**. Cut voice input, image generation, custom themes, long conversations and automatic idea research. Keep the no-idea route, editable output, publication, a response and persistence. **Do not start this phase with a handoff dashboard.**

### Phase 2 — Help a launch get seen and used

**Ships:** founder/idea pages, short updates, a curated Starting now shelf, following, the distribution kit, a small consented need board, functional service filters and natural-language interpretation where available. Connect one actual OpenSeat class/booking journey, including a truthful waitlist and customer activity. Other templates can take inquiries while their adapters are incomplete. A small curated feedback circle can use the same invitation/inbox primitives.

**Underneath:** separate searchable public projections, authorized class publication and offer linkage, offer availability, deterministic constraints, contact consent, attribution, customer-to-reference association, moderation/reporting and the OpenSeat adapter. The current HTTP API does not yet expose a founder class-publishing workflow; build its authorization rather than expose the internal setup method. Customer booking references are access-bearing secrets in the current service; keep them out of public content and shared logs. The displayed action must agree with whether payment is actually supported.

**Tony's phone finish:** post a fictional need, discover a suitable new offer, submit an inquiry or make a supported free/test booking, see the founder receive it, and return through My activity. Share the storefront link manually, then inspect the attributed response. Open an empty search and deliberately change one constraint.

**Done when:** an unfamiliar visitor can find and use an offer; a new founder can be found without historical revenue; availability and action labels are truthful; private searches do not appear as public needs; a last-seat race yields one confirmed place and an accurate alternative.

**Size / cut:** **L**. Cut map views, algorithmic feeds, email automation and automatic cohort matching. Keep useful pages, a working customer action, response consent and a credible first distribution route.

### Phase 3 — Let the new business take a real testnet payment

**Ships:** approachable signing setup, sponsored supported operations, a newly deployed locked business version, one accepted test token, one paid order type, recorded customer receipts, refunds and a simple founder money view. The order product is fully usable before adding subscription automation.

**Underneath:** factory/provisioning, independent controlling identities, recovery path, relay controls, allowed FT integration, order registration, capacity holds, payment/reserve accounting, callbacks, finality-aware indexing and persisted operation recovery. Preserve and verify the two-party sale invariants in v2 now; its closing UI can come later. Existing v1 records remain linked as their own history.

**Tony's phone finish:** activate a fictional business, authorize a supported test payment as a separate customer, return to its confirmed booking and source receipt, then cancel under the stated policy and follow the refund. Lose the browser connection during submission and resume the same operation without a duplicate payment.

**Done when:** the amount is actually backed by the accepted test token, the wrong-token path fails, duplicate events do not double credit, unknown outcomes reconcile, and protected funds cannot be withdrawn as available cash. The phone approval/recovery behavior has been exercised. Source badges state testnet and no commercial value.

**Size / cut:** **XL**, split into account/provisioning, paid order, and refund/recovery checkpoints, each phone-reviewable. Cut extra assets, recurring mandates, conversion and foreign-chain actions. Do not substitute v1 DEMO minting for the payment integration.

### Phase 4 — Make growth and potential value visible

**Ships:** the daily desk, next-offer workflow, customer outcomes and source attribution, one scoped delegation, basic cost entry and the first **Value** screen. Include early estimates, manual assumption edits, source explanations, “what changed” and saved scenarios. Seeded multi-period examples are labeled separately from newly observed test payments.

**Underneath:** task/outcome projections, scoped operator integration, receipt-to-order classification, earned-period rules, cost provenance and deterministic model/version storage. Build the small financial definitions the calculator needs. A full accounting suite and comparable-sales database are not dependencies.

**Tony's phone finish:** receive a customer task, delegate a supported action, publish the next offer, open its Value screen, inspect one receipt-derived input, change replacement labor and reproduce the new range. Compare a short-history business with an idea that has only a value plan. Keep the scenario private.

**Done when:** the number can be recomputed; all illustrative multiples and annualization assumptions are visible; a payment has not become profit by default; a correction changes a new snapshot rather than accepted past evidence; a revoked operator cannot continue privileged work. A founder can see a next growth action and a value explanation in the same session.

**Size / cut:** **L** across several narrow slices. Cut predictive ML, elaborate forecasts, bank connectors and a complete runbook engine. Keep the useful estimate, explicit costs, scenarios, a customer task and one working delegation.

### Phase 5 — Give acquirers a marketplace worth searching

**Ships:** buying profiles/teams, mandates, seller opt-in, acquisition listings, stage/revenue/value/asking-price filters, shortlist/compare, saved criteria, scoped access requests and a first evidence room. Valuation is a visible comparison tool from this phase, not a future placeholder.

**Underneath:** buyer roles, disclosure previews, separate acquisition index, grant-aware fields/counts/exports, metric snapshots and private buyer scenarios. Alerts can begin inside the application. Anonymous teasers use only their published bands.

**Tony's phone finish:** create a fictional Northline buying brief; find OpenSeat on its selected category and asking budget; compare value assumptions; request seven-day access to aggregate financial evidence; approve a narrowed grant as the authorized seller; inspect a reproducible metric as buyer. Expire or revoke the grant and see access end.

**Done when:** a service shopper cannot discover private acquisition status; an unapproved buying team cannot fetch the room; range filters name their model type and leave unknown candidates accessible; asking price and estimated value remain separate; permission checks cover the API as well as the UI.

**Size / cut:** **L**. Cut advanced natural-language mandates, external adviser integrations, outbound alerts and broad connector coverage. Keep meaningful search, the estimate, one comparison and one scoped room.

### Phase 6 — Let two people accept a deal and keep serving customers

**Ships:** scope/terms editor, frozen evidence packet, changed-terms review, buyer funding, both acceptances, visible settlement state, seller proceeds withdrawal and buyer first shift. Connect the current proven control model through the supported payment-aware contract. Include an explicit outstanding external transfer item when one remains.

**Underneath:** digest construction, separate party approvals, authority generation changes, handoff barriers, asynchronous withdrawals, competing-deal recovery and customer entitlement continuity. Retain the existing v1 testnet result as history; it does not stand in for validation of the new token-backed version.

**Tony's phone finish:** as seller, review exact terms; with a separately controlled fictional buyer identity, fund and accept the same version; observe the confirmed control change; appoint a buyer worker; use a pre-sale customer reference. Attempt a former-owner action and see the actual refusal. Inspect proceeds and the separate external-transfer status.

**Done when:** revised terms require reacceptance; settlement cannot happen twice; reserved customer funds stay protected; former owner/operator authority ends; customer rights survive; payout and external-handoff failures stay visible and recoverable. The next customer action remains a product action, not a film sequence.

**Size / cut:** **L**, with substantial contract verification inherited from phase 3. Cut financing, earn-outs, fractional interests and automated provider transfers. Keep the exact agreement, independent approvals, actual control change and the buyer serving an existing customer.

### Phase 7 — Give more kinds of founder a complete business

**Ships sequentially:** a full scoped-service adapter, then a physical-product adapter, then deeper software renewal support as demand warrants. Each gets a separate phone checkpoint before the next begins. Launch guidance, storefront, finder and Value already recognize these families; now their operational depth grows.

**Underneath:** service scope/revisions/milestones; goods stock, shipping, returns and address privacy; prepaid/capped subscription mandates and scheduled workers. Contract changes use a new deployment version as described in section 6; do not assume the phase-3 account can be upgraded. Add category-specific valuation inputs such as replacement labor, equipment or inventory without pretending they are interchangeable.

**Tony's phone finishes:** accept and deliver a fictional fixed-scope service with a revision; order a fictional product and follow its explicitly labeled test shipment/return state; renew and cancel a test software period. Each checkpoint also supports a new founder publishing that type through the launch flow. Physical delivery remains a provider simulation until an authorized real adapter and pilot exist.

**Done when:** each template has an actual completed action and a remedy, with its own authority and financial effects; unsupported provider actions cannot appear complete. A person looking for a product or service can understand the terms without knowing the underlying adapter.

**Size / cut:** **L** per adapter, **XL** in aggregate. Cut category breadth before shipping a buy button with no fulfillment or remedy. The existing launch/inquiry product for other categories stays useful.

### Phase 8 — Make the launch network compound

**Ships one experiment at a time:** nonfinancial demand-backed launches, better First Five circles, partner shop windows, reusable business recipes and clearer founder stories. Improve estimate calibration with suitable evidence as a separate, measurable workstream. Add Intents or Chain Signatures only for an actual checkout or operating need.

**Underneath:** conditional-interest state, consent, moderation, partner/referral rules, licensed templates, model evaluation and repeatable export/restore. Track whether the new capability helps first orders and repeat service, not just public activity.

**Tony's phone finish:** launch a fictional workshop conditional on enough interest, collect explicit test responses, approve opening it and invite separate bookings. In a later checkpoint, start a different fictional business from a recipe and successfully edit its promises rather than inherit another founder's claims.

**Done when:** each launch experiment has a useful measured outcome and an honest failure state; copied templates carry no customers or secrets; no interest is converted into a purchase without a new choice. Before any approved real-world rollout, exercise the production decisions in section 6, including recovery, support and independent review.

**Size / cut:** **M–L** per experiment. Cut a broad social network, automatic investment products and speculative integrations. Choose the next experiment from observed founder friction.

### Risks and the cuts that preserve this idea

| Risk | Practical response | Cut first if scope bites |
|---|---|---|
| A beautiful launch flow ends at a page nobody sees | Make the share route, useful inquiry and reply task part of the first two phases; be explicit about internal traffic | Feed personalization and elaborate branding tools |
| The founder becomes a copy reviewer instead of an entrepreneur | Keep every AI output attached to a customer, deliverable or experiment; allow direct editing | Long generated plans and autonomous “business agents” |
| The network rewards hype or manufactured traction | Distinguish interest, paid activity and fulfillment; moderate and flag related transactions | Popularity metrics and automatic broad distribution |
| The valuation absorbs the entire build | Ship an explainable model and editable assumptions on the first useful financial substrate | Comparable-data automation, elaborate forecasts and general accounting |
| Payments delay the launch product | Keep drafts, profiles, inquiries and working free actions independent of commerce activation | Multi-asset checkout and subscription automation |
| More business types exceed fulfillment support | Support launch and inquiry broadly, then complete one adapter at a time | Breadth of paid fulfillment, without removing non-software founders from the product |
| Immutable code or off-chain hosting weakens later operations | Preserve deployment lineage, bounded calls, customer claims and export/restore; disclose who operates the service | Unnecessary contract modules and external-chain features |

The spine to preserve is **shape an offer → publish it → reach someone → deliver → grow → understand value → optionally find a buyer**. Phase 1 must already feel like Everstreet, and phase 4 should show why building here becomes more valuable over time.

## 10. Five decisions for Tony

These are proposed defaults for the design, not requests to pause the reversible build.

1. **Should the homepage and next build lead with starting a business?** My recommendation: **yes**—“Start your business here,” with all four entrances visible and the guided launch as phase 1. Keep the warm paper/forest visual direction, with the founder's work and offer as the hero. This intentionally reorders the earlier customer-first homepage recommendation.
2. **Where should the first community have depth?** My recommendation: **creative services, workshops and the small businesses supporting them**. Support all four launch families from the beginning, but use Juniper/OpenSeat and adjacent fictional businesses for the first complete customer loop. This is a first neighborhood, not the platform's category boundary.
3. **How visible should valuation be, and how early?** My recommendation: **a private founder Value tab in phase 4 and a seller-controlled acquisition search feature in phase 5**. Ship useful scenario-based ranges early, with sources and assumptions beside them. Keep asking price separate; no public valuation leaderboard and no pressure to sell.
4. **What should someone get free, and what might they pay for?** My recommendation: **a founder profile, idea testing, one useful storefront and first-customer workflow free**, then test payment for growing operations, additional capacity/team tools and transparent commerce/acquisition services. Avoid a mandatory paid plan before a founder has received value; never sell organic ranking or evidence status. Exact prices follow observed costs and usefulness.
5. **Which bold launch experiment should follow the core?** My recommendation: **“Six people and we'll open,” using revocable nonfinancial interest first**. It connects an aspiring founder directly with demand, works for classes, services and product batches, and gives discovery a role before inventory or a full business exists.
