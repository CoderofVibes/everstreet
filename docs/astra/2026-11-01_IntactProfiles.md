# Everstreet: built to change hands intact

Astra product and visual direction for Tony and Claude · 2026-11-01  
An extension of [Start your business here](2026-11-01_LaunchPad.md). Proposal only; Tony decides scope and release, Claude owns implementation.

**Start your business here. Build something that can keep its promises when you move on.**

Keep the launch pad: the aspiring entrepreneur, the useful first offer, the workshop, the first customer, the daily work and the possibility of growth. Give that journey a durable center. From its first paid operation, the business has its own account, its own money, its own customer commitments and published limits on what any owner may do. Its profile grows from that activity. When someone buys it, they take over an operating business with a history and responsibilities.

**The living business profile is the front door to that same business.** What it does, what customers receive, how reliably it delivers, what it earns, what it costs to run and Everstreet's estimate of its value belong together. A founder should be proud to share it. A customer should be able to use it. A buyer should be able to examine it. It should get more useful with every month the business operates, even if it never goes up for sale.

The memorable acquisition scene is a developer's API continuing to answer requests while its owner changes. The endpoint, customer credentials, prepaid balances, accepted terms and service history remain. The outgoing founder loses control. The incoming owner has an approved team ready to work. The same design can let the customers buy the service they depend on.

The profile's headline is **real operating numbers computed by Everstreet, with an explanation of where each came from**. Revenue, overhead, operating profit, performance and estimated value deserve prominent space. Publication is the founder's choice. An impressive private operating desk is as much the product as an impressive public page.

All people, businesses, usage and financial figures below are fictional design examples. USD figures describe a future reporting interface, not redeemable test balances or observed revenue. A development screen must carry its actual environment label and supported test asset. Sizes are relative: S = contained change; M = short workflow; L = substantial product slice; XL = subsystem to split into checkpoints. They are not time estimates.

## 1. The thread from launch to succession

### What this adds to the launch-pad pass

| Keep from the launch pad | Extend in this pass |
|---|---|
| Start first; Shape → Test → Launch → Grow; four entrances | Make business identity, customer promises and eventual continuity part of the launch itself |
| Founder, idea and storefront pages | Grow the storefront into a living business profile with operations, economics, performance, rules and history |
| Value as an early founder feature and acquisition tool | Put selected financial results and the Everstreet valuation estimate prominently on the profile, with field-level disclosure |
| OpenSeat, Juniper, workshops, services and goods | Add a profitable API as the most complete handover example; keep the existing businesses and their customer journeys |
| Following, useful updates and First Five | Make following an ongoing relationship with a business; distinguish product, learning, financial and governance updates |
| Exact managed-rights transfer and surviving customer obligations | Add funded usage balances, workers, rules, operating costs and a staged successor team; rehearse the API staying available |

This pass does not rename or revise the earlier pass. Its new defaults are proposals for Tony, particularly the API's flagship role and how prominently founders are invited to share financials.

### Four things a business starts with

Drafting an idea stays easy. Publishing an idea or collecting nonfinancial interest requires no business contract. **Activating the operating business** creates the following before its first supported paid order:

| What the founder sees | What it means | What it needs / size |
|---|---|---|
| **Your business has its own account** | “You control it. Its money and customer commitments stay with the business if ownership changes.” A readable name leads to the NEAR identity and controlling account | Stable platform business ID, confirmed per-business deployment, independent owner identity, deployment/version record; L with provisioning |
| **Money for running the business** | Payments enter its treasury. Customer credit, money owed and money available to spend are shown separately. The founder's personal funds and eventual sale proceeds are separate | Accepted asset, categorized balances, reserves, funding/withdrawal status; XL with payment work |
| **Promises every owner must keep** | A short set of customer protections, their enforcement and who can change them. The founder previews the actual purchase receipt | Versioned rule templates, original terms on each entitlement, machine-enforced policies where supported; L |
| **People and services that do the work** | The founder appoints a server worker, support person or fulfillment operator with a job, limits, cost and end date | Scoped grants, pay agreements, operating adapter, revocation and handoff behavior; L |

The simple activation copy is:

> Your business gets its own NEAR account. It holds customer payments and keeps track of what you owe. You choose who can operate it. Any future owner takes over with the customer promises shown here still in place.

The next screen shows a concrete example: **“A customer pays 50. Until you deliver or refund it, that 50 is protected.”** Adapt the example to the offer and asset. A workshop can reserve a paid place; an API can hold refundable usage credit. The contract must actually support the selected policy before the interface offers it.

Let someone save the choices and return. Activation is complete only after the account, supported rules, payment path and first fulfillment route are ready. Do not call a draft “on NEAR.” In the long-term product, “born to change hands intact” describes an activated business; an imported business instead shows which parts have been connected and which still depend on its founder.

Keep the distinction between a person's login and the business's account. Recovering a former founder's personal login must not recover ownership of a sold business. A stable profile URL can link deployment history, but cannot move funds or obligations out of an old locked contract. The current account-version constraint in AUTHORITY.md remains.

### While the business runs

Give the founder a quiet **Business can keep going** panel in Today, beneath customer work and the next growth action. Use specific statements rather than a universal readiness score:

- **Customers protected:** 5,000 of unused credit held; refund requests current.
- **Work covered:** serving worker active; support covered through Friday.
- **One dependency to resolve:** only Nadia can deploy a release.
- **Last rehearsal:** a second operator restored the service from a backup on the stated date.
- **Profile current:** October financials ready to review; one vendor bill still unclassified.

Tap the deployment dependency and get an actual task: create the business-owned release package, give a second operator bounded release access, and run a rehearsal. It helps a founder take a holiday today and gives a buyer evidence later. Nothing needs to be listed for sale.

**Needs / size:** dependency register attached to real tasks, coverage/expiry projections, evidence-linked rehearsal records, financial exceptions and the existing Today surface; L in small slices. Start with one replaceable worker and one founder-only task. Do not substitute a percentage for the unresolved dependency.

### When ownership changes

The name and profile persist. The ownership history gains a new chapter. Customer terms and original receipts persist. Followers who chose to follow the business still follow it. The buyer gains the supported operating rights; the founder's personal profile, other projects, private notes and personal followers remain theirs.

An existing customer should encounter **“Same service. Your balance and terms remain.”** where an ownership notice is useful. Their application should not need a new endpoint or API key solely because of the sale. Where a provider really requires a migration or customer consent, show that requirement and complete it; continuity is a result to demonstrate for the stated scope.

This is the difference a founder can feel from day one: delivery, receipts, rules, delegation and the profile accumulate in the same business record. A sale uses those records and that authority. It does not require inventing an operational identity at listing time. Acquire.com's transfer guide illustrates the separate hosting, code, billing and customer-data work that conventional SaaS transfers can involve; our design inference is to establish as much business-scoped infrastructure as possible before a sale. It does not remove provider dependencies by declaration. [Acquire.com SaaS asset transfer guide](https://help.acquire.com/saas-asset-transfer-guide)

**The product promise is strongest where Everstreet manages the whole operating path.** NEAR can enforce supported money and control rules. Servers deliver the API; people do support; providers host code and data. The profile identifies those parties and the evidence for each claim. “Built on NEAR” must never expand into “every asset, customer and service result is on chain.”

## 2. The flagship: Paperfin API, from first offer to its next owner

### A business someone can understand in one sentence

**Paperfin API turns order data into consistent PDF receipts for small software businesses.** Nadia Vale built it because every small commerce tool seemed to need a receipt renderer. Customers send structured fictional order data to a stable endpoint and receive a document. The initial offer charges **$0.01 for each successful billable render**, funded by prepaid credit. Failed renders are not billed. Free trials have a separate allowance and never count as paying customers.

Choose a bounded, self-contained renderer for the first example: versioned templates, local rendering and storage, without an essential upstream paid API or secret personal account. This reduces the number of external conditions the first complete handover must satisfy. Keep OpenSeat independently runnable and keep Juniper's workshop discoverable. Paperfin demonstrates the deepest software continuity; OpenSeat demonstrates continuity across business tools and real customer activities.

### The journey the platform must support

| Moment | What Nadia or the next person sees and does | What accumulates in the business |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Shape the offer** | Nadia chooses Software → Usage API in the existing launch studio. She describes the customer, imports or creates an authorized code artifact, sets the per-render price and runs a sample request | Editable overview, API documentation, offer version, sample output, deployment checklist and a founder introduction |
| **2. Activate** | “Set up Paperfin's account and payments.” Review independent controlling identity, one settlement asset, supported refund/rate rules and public-chain disclosures | Confirmed business account, empty treasury, published rule set, deployment identity and stable profile URL |
| **3. Put the work in the business** | Appoint a renderer worker and a human support operator. See each capability, payment basis and budget. Attach the release artifact and runbook | Generation-bound grants; business-scoped code, configuration and data permissions; approved worker agreements; named hosting dependency |
| **4. Serve the first paid customer** | Fictional Cedar Cart prepays $50, sees 5,000 renders at the accepted rate, creates a customer credential and runs its integration | Accepted payment, $50 refundable liability, customer-owned entitlement, term version and a successful metered result |
| **5. Run and grow** | Nadia resolves failures, pays support, publishes a new template, watches retained customers and reviews the monthly figures | Usage, recognized revenue, worker costs, overhead, refunds, performance, incidents and useful profile updates |
| **6. Make the founder replaceable** | A second operator deploys and restores the service in an isolated rehearsal. The profile shows what was actually exercised | Dated continuity evidence, failed steps, dependency changes and a better estimate of replacement work |
| **7. Let a buyer inspect** | Nadia opts into acquisition discovery. Northline receives a scoped room and can reproduce earnings, examine obligations and try the renderer in a sandbox | A versioned evidence packet, permitted cost/customer detail, transfer manifest, buyer scenarios and questions |
| **8. Prepare and close** | Both parties review identical terms, fresh readiness evidence, price, treasury treatment and the successor worker plan. Buyer funding is separate | Accepted deal version, confirmed control change, new generation, seller claim, continuing reserves and staged operating authority |
| **9. Keep answering** | Cedar Cart sends another request using its existing integration. Northline sees its first shift and the next scheduled worker payment | A post-close result under the new authority, the same customer account and rate, and an ownership-history entry |

**Needs / size:** reuse launch, profile, money and deal primitives; add a separately runnable API adapter, usage-credit flow, business-scoped deployment artifact and bounded workers. XL, split between the first payment, a metered customer action, worker payment and continuity rehearsal. This is a new proposed example, not an existing repository capability.

### The business, the worker and the customer are different principals

The business collects customer money and owes delivery or a refund. A rendering process receives a narrow right to serve and report supported usage. A support person receives a narrow right to handle cases. Neither becomes the owner merely because they perform work. Their agreed pay comes from the business's available funds or a separately reserved payable; unused customer credit remains protected.

Every worker card should answer: **What job? What can it change? What does it cost? Who receives payment? What happens if it stops?** A server agreement might use metered compute up to a monthly cap. A person might invoice approved support hours. A worker cannot submit an arbitrary cost and immediately pay itself. Human acceptance, a bounded contract rate or a named service attestation must determine what becomes owed.

For Paperfin, offer a managed-runtime path: the code artifact, configuration, customer credential store, backups and service routing live in a business-scoped environment from launch. An ownership change changes authorized control of that environment. Everstreet or its hosting provider remains an explicit trusted operator of it. The buyer receives the supported control and export/restore path; a cloud-provider account does not magically change legal owner.

Customer credentials authorize consumption of customer entitlements. Operator credentials authorize running or changing the business. Keep them separate. Do not revoke every customer key when revoking Nadia's worker. Do rotate or replace seller-controlled operational secrets where they could bypass the new authority. A code export must not export an old founder's personal credentials.

### Prepayment is a promise, then usage earns it

For the simple offer, $1 buys the right to 100 successful billable renders at the accepted rate. Display both remaining funds and remaining usage at that rate. Preserve the money amount, asset and price version; “credit” must not hide whether the customer can redeem it.

Before a request can spend credit, reserve or otherwise authorize a bounded amount against that customer's spendable balance. Only one successful settlement can consume the request's charge. Release the hold on a confirmed nonbillable result. Retries of the same request do not create a second bill. Show in-progress usage separately from credit available to withdraw.

The customer can request withdrawal of unused, unheld credit without owner permission. If a request is still in progress, show its small hold and bounded expiry; an owner must not keep credit hostage with indefinite usage holds. Detailed batching, authorization and reconciliation are Claude's design. The product requires bounded loss exposure, a customer-visible record, a correction path and a named source for delivery assertions. A blockchain receipt alone cannot prove that the PDF was useful or even delivered.

### A close that genuinely leaves the client alone

The target is a running request stream across the sale, with a particular old customer credential and old funded balance. Do not demonstrate only that a replacement client can log in afterward.

1. **Rehearse.** Northline sees the package version, restores a copy, performs a release and completes a support task using allowed synthetic data. Readiness evidence names what was tested, when and by whom.
2. **Stage the next team.** Northline approves the exact worker capabilities, accounts, budgets and effective generation in the deal's operating plan. The plan grants no pre-close operating power to Northline. Nadia cannot choose an unreviewed successor on its behalf.
3. **Establish the cutover boundary.** The service stops admitting new old-generation privileged work at its handoff barrier, reconciles in-flight mutations and records the transition state. Customer requests continue only through an authority path shown to remain valid. If no safe serving path exists, postpone closing or show the expected interruption.
4. **Close the supported scope.** Buyer funding settles to the seller's internal claim, control changes and the authority generation advances. The customer reserves and terms stay. Bind any next-generation grant installation to the accepted plan; engineering may use a staged activation mechanism, but cannot leave old grants secretly valid to make the demo smooth.
5. **Confirm and serve.** Once the new state is final and the service has activated the approved serving path, record requests handled under the new generation. Ongoing customer authentication uses the same credential and entitlement. A trusted platform runtime may bridge the controlled cutover only within an explicitly designed authority policy; this is not already established by gate 1.
6. **Prove the boundary.** The old founder's privileged call fails. The old worker's new discretionary command fails. The new worker succeeds. A previously committed refund or earned worker payable still resolves once to its original beneficiary through the settlement path; revoking discretion must not erase liabilities.

In the customer trace, Cedar Cart has **$18.42** before the close and sends five new successful billable renders afterward. Its balance becomes **$18.37** at the same price. The trace joins request IDs, term version, authorized usage, receipt and authority generation privately; it never publishes a bearer credential. The aggregate closing ledger below is a snapshot before those five calls.

Show the observed latency and errors around the transition. “No interruption observed in this run” is evidence about that run. A general uptime guarantee requires its own service design and funded remedy. If the UI, settlement or hosting fails, preserve the customer's claim and show the actual state. Do not animate a successful answer.

The sale example uses an **illustrative $195,000 price**, paid from Northline's separate acquisition funds. The $14,000 business treasury stays with Paperfin, including $5,000 of protected unused customer credit. In this particular deal, the other $9,000 is agreed working cash included in the price. It is not added to the estimate or paid to the seller a second time. Required closing cash and restrictions on pre-close withdrawals belong in the accepted terms.

### The second ending: the customers buy Paperfin

Ten of Paperfin's 48 active paying customer accounts organize the fictional **Paperfin Customer Cooperative**. They need the service to remain dependable and want a say in its future. They remain customers while evaluating it; membership gives no early access to other customers' records.

| Step | What the cooperative experience adds | Boundary and requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Form a buying group | “Acquire with the people who use it.” Invite named members into a private buying workspace; describe purpose, proposed operating budget and governance | Invitations and interest are not investment commitments. “Cooperative” in the example describes the intended organization; any actual legal form and agreements are separate work |
| Examine the same business | Inspect the same revenue, costs, concentration, founder work, reserves, rules and tested handover | Members see the aggregate buyer grant. A member cannot view another customer's usage or balance by joining the group |
| Agree how to govern | Example: one admitted member business, one vote; six of ten approvals for acquisition and specified owner actions; two named operators handle bounded daily work | Membership, quorum, proposal expiry, signer control, recovery and membership-change rules must be explicit. Ordinary API calls never wait for a membership vote |
| Fund the agreed acquisition | In this fictional scenario each contributes $19,500, making $195,000 of separately accounted acquisition funding | These funds are not Paperfin customer revenue. Existing customer credit cannot be repurposed as a contribution. A failed/expired purchase needs a defined refund path |
| Accept and close | The group-controlled NEAR account makes the buyer acceptance after the required approvals; the seller accepts the same terms | A group badge or app vote is insufficient. The controlling account must enforce the chosen group authority. An async group call must still bind the current deal version and expiry |
| Operate together | Profile: “Customer-owned since [date].” Publish an approved operating plan and selected governance updates; keep the service and customer receipts working | The business still pays its workers, reserves unused credit and serves nonmembers. Customer protection rules constrain the cooperative too |
| Change a representative later | A member rotates its representative through the specified governance/recovery path; new operators can be appointed | Personal departure does not require transferring every customer balance or reissuing all API credentials. Governance liveness must be rehearsed |

Give the group a concrete first-month decision: **retain the current price while funding a second serving worker**. The Value screen can show the cost of that choice and compare buying with continuing as a customer or migrating elsewhere. The cooperative's strategic value is a separate buyer scenario; Everstreet must not inflate the public estimate merely because the group depends on the service.

The other 38 active paying customer accounts remain customers with the same published rights. Cooperative membership may eventually carry additional benefits, but does not silently convert a prepaid balance into an ownership interest, permit access to others' data, or authorize a majority to withdraw protected funds.

**Needs / size:** buying-team workflow and aggregate evidence, group-controlled account adapter, binding approvals and bounded funding/refund handling; L–XL beyond the single-buyer close. First prove a group-owned account can accept and later appoint/revoke an operator in a fictional sandbox/testnet journey. Pooled funding infrastructure and a real cooperative offering are later scope decisions. Do not delay the single-buyer API path while inventing a general investment platform.

## 3. The living business profile

### One business, several authorized readings

The business profile becomes the stable destination linked from an offer, a founder's personal page, a receipt, an update and an acquisition result. It has a recognizable identity and a working customer action throughout its life. It should feel like a business you can get to know through its work.

Recommended sections: **Overview · Offers · Performance · Customers · Financials · Value · People · Owner rules · Operations · Updates**. The full transfer manifest belongs in the buyer room. On a phone, show Overview and a short section picker; do not force ten tabs into one horizontal strip. A customer receipt opens directly to **Your service**, balance, support and the rules that apply to that customer.

Nadia's personal “about me” remains a personal profile linked from **People**. Paperfin keeps the truthful attribution “Founded by Nadia Vale” after sale, while “Run by” updates to the current owner and team. The seller's other ideas, personal messages and private follower relationships do not become buyer assets.

### What generates and maintains each section

“Automatic” below means a deterministic projection from permitted, supported records. The founder chooses disclosure rules. AI may propose narrative; it cannot invent missing figures or convert an unsupported claim into verified evidence.

| Section | Everstreet generates and maintains | Founder or current operator writes | AI drafts | Freshness shown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Overview** | Business stage, active offers, current operating status, selected metric strip, founding/current-control dates | Purpose, audience, positioning, description, imagery and factual corrections | Short introduction from approved offer fields; a proposed monthly summary | Status observed at; copy last approved at; each metric's own period |
| **Offers / product** | Supported actions, current price/version, availability, trial and documentation links, published service terms | What it does, exclusions, examples, release descriptions | Documentation explanations, package descriptions and onboarding copy | Live availability observation; offer version/effective date; stale state when source fails |
| **Performance** | Defined success/error rates, latency distribution, incident periods, usage and monitor coverage from the adapter | Service targets, planned maintenance context, incident explanation and resolution | A grounded account of changes and affected periods | Telemetry through [time], window and missing intervals; source type |
| **Customers** | Qualifying active/paying accounts, retention cohorts, repeat use, concentration and approved review links | Customer segments, supported use cases; permissioned case studies and named references | Aggregate customer narrative; case-study draft from consented material | Cohort cutoff, account definition, review date and consent status |
| **People / about me** | Links between founding person, current owner, appointed team and supported roles | Personal bio, motivation, relevant experience, team introductions and availability | An editable bio from the person's own answers; interview-based founder story | Person last confirmed; role observed at; employment/history claims separately sourced |
| **Financials** | Receipts, earned revenue, direct costs, overhead, operating profit, liabilities, refunds and cash views from classified records | Off-platform costs, completeness declaration, corrections, accounting-policy choices and period commentary | Explanation of the computed bridge and unresolved items | Period end; sources reconciled through; calculated at; provisional/closed/revised version |
| **Value** | Versioned Everstreet estimate, model inputs, range, coverage and changes; reproducible saved scenarios | Private labor/cost assumptions, selected disclosure, separate asking price if selling | Plain explanation and labeled what-if ideas from actual inputs | Input cutoff and calculation date; model version/date; stale-input warning |
| **Owner rules** | Plain-language rendering of supported rule fields, versions, beneficiaries, effective dates, enforcement source and limits | Choices among supported policies; separately labeled additional commitments | Proposed wording with a diff against structured terms | Current authority observed at; immutable accepted version on customer receipt |
| **Operations / continuity** | Authorized roles, worker status and pay basis, documented dependencies, rehearsal results, backup/restore observations | Runbooks, off-platform provider relationships, founder workload and succession notes | Draft runbooks from approved procedures; proposed missing-dependency tasks | Each observation and rehearsal date; expiries; unknown dependencies remain unknown |
| **Updates / history** | Candidate milestones from dated events; ownership and published-policy history; links to source-backed period summaries | Lessons, launches, plans, context and approval of optional public updates | Monthly business letter, learning post or release-note draft | Event time, publication time and correction/version history |

### Audience is a product feature

The product exposes four understandable audience choices: **Public**, **Followers**, **Approved buyers**, **Private team**. An approved buyer means a named buyer/team with an active, scoped seller grant. Identity or eligibility verification is a separate badge; a “verified buyer” does not automatically obtain evidence. The customer also has a separate right to their own balance, receipts and accepted rules, regardless of these publication settings.

| Section | Public default | Followers, if the founder chooses | Approved buyer detail, only when granted | Private team / customer-specific detail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overview | Approved description, stage and working offer | Early product/learning updates | Permitted acquisition context | Draft strategy; private launch interview |
| Offers | Published offers, supported action and customer terms | Release previews or launch alerts | Product economics and version history within scope | Draft pricing; each customer's entitlement and private payload |
| Performance | A selected service-health summary and incident route | Product/performance digest | Raw aggregate windows, coverage, incident evidence and exclusions | Operational logs; customer sees its own request history |
| Customers | Segment description and consented references; counts optional | Approved customer stories | Aggregate cohorts and concentration; identities only under a distinct justified grant | CRM, contact details, private feedback; each customer sees only its own activity |
| People | Approved bio, founding attribution and published current team | Personal stories only if separately chosen | Workload, responsibility and founder-dependency evidence | Personal notes, private team details and unrelated projects |
| Financials | Off by default; offer a deliberate “Share my numbers” preview | Optional rounded monthly digest | Granted periods, classifications, missing costs and source evidence | Full operating ledger and imports; each customer sees its own receipts |
| Value | Off by default; publish exact range or chosen band with model label | Approved value/learning digest | Shared estimate plus calculation; buyer's own private scenario | Founder scenarios and unpublished asking price |
| Owner rules | Rules relevant to a public offer, enforcement and modification limits | Notices of published changes | Full supported rule configuration, obligations and rule history | Draft rules; a customer's personal terms and beneficiary details |
| Operations | Selected supported continuity statements and dependencies | Optional behind-the-scenes updates | Granted worker costs, runbooks, transfer limitations and rehearsal evidence | Secrets never rendered; operational credentials stay in their authorized store |
| Updates | Approved public posts and factual public history | Posts intentionally addressed to followers | Deal-relevant changes in the room | Unapproved AI drafts, private comments and private deal status |

Following is a low-trust audience choice. Do not put customer names, sensitive financial evidence or acquisition negotiations behind a follow button and treat them as private. The UI explains that anyone allowed to follow may read and retain that material. Exact buyer documents use named, expiring grants.

Hide undisclosed figures completely: no blurred value, inferred chart axis, locked customer count, ranking side channel or public placeholder announcing a private sale. Financial visibility and acquisition openness are independent. A founder can run an openly measured business without inviting offers.

### What each visitor gets in one minute

| Reader | First minute on Paperfin | Useful next action |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer** | “Will this render my receipts? What does it cost? Is it working? Can I get unused money back? What survives a sale?” See sample, price, recent performance and applicable owner rules before optional financial detail | Run a trial, fund supported usage, inspect personal balance or get support |
| **Fellow founder** | “What did Nadia build, whom does it help, what did she learn, and what might help my business?” See approved story, operating choices, selected numbers and dated experiments | Follow product/learning updates, ask a scoped question, use or adapt a shared recipe |
| **Acquirer** | “What produces these earnings, how complete are the costs, what must continue, and what would I actually control?” Enter the granted buyer view with a source-backed financial bridge, rule burden, dependencies and rehearsal | Reproduce one material number, change a private assumption, request a missing category or inspect exact transfer scope |

The business does not write three inconsistent biographies. Shared factual components render different permitted fields and actions. The buyer view must state its audience and grant expiry and provide **View public profile**. A founder's **Preview as...** uses those same server projections.

### Maintenance that saves actual work

Each field has a source mode: **From business records**, **Written by us**, or **AI draft awaiting review**. Sourced amounts cannot be overwritten by typing a better number into the profile. Correct a misclassified transaction or disputed usage at its source, keep the correction history, then recompute. A founder can hide an optional public metric, but cannot relabel its source or silently select a flattering slice while calling it the standard period.

Allow automatic refresh only for the fields and audiences the founder explicitly enabled. A public “last completed month” card advances when that period is reconciled under its stated policy. If it is incomplete, it says provisional or keeps the previous period with its date. A buyer can request the current incomplete period separately. A frozen post or accepted evidence packet retains the published snapshot; a live profile card can link to the newer revision.

Proposed initial freshness budgets: monetary/authority projections update after confirmed observations and normally reach the UI within 60 seconds; supported operational telemetry rolls every five minutes; financial month summaries rebuild nightly and after material corrections; estimates recompute daily when inputs change. These are engineering targets to measure, not established service levels. Display observation time and computation time separately. Detect missed expected updates, show **Delayed** or **Source disconnected**, and keep the last dated result. A missing observation is never zero revenue or perfect uptime.

On sale, migrate business publication authority to the new controlling owner, revoke seller administrative sessions and remove old grant/cached access as appropriate. Preserve the business's permitted public history and reader subscriptions. Historical consent for a named customer quote still has to be respected; ownership does not broaden its permitted use. Preview and confirm future auto-publication settings as part of the buyer's first shift.

**Needs / size:** profile schema, field provenance, audience projections, subscriptions, approved narrative versions, metric snapshots, source health and ownership-aware administration; L for the living profile, with money/performance integrations delivered in their own slices. Start with useful overview/offers/story/rules and honest empty financial states, then populate from the same reusable components as the operating desk.

## 4. Real numbers are a headline

### The financial headline the product should earn

For an operating business that chooses to share, put these together near the top of its profile:

> **October: $12,000 revenue · $900 overhead · $7,500 operating profit**  
> $3,600 direct delivery costs · 99.80% successful requests  
> **Everstreet valuation estimate: $156k–$234k**  
> Scenario model · 3 complete months annualized · fictional example  
> Revenue from metered delivery; costs include supplied records. **See numbers and sources**

Overhead and direct costs both appear so the visitor can understand the profit. At phone width use two rows of cards, then the valuation card; do not shrink this into a six-number ticker. Value is large enough to notice, with the word **estimate** attached to the amount. A published band still carries its model, period and source limitations.

For a new founder show **First offer live**, **First customer action** and **Build a value plan**. If no financial period exists, say so. Do not assign a made-up valuation or turn missing costs into profit. Revenue that is observed while a material cost is unknown can still be displayed; the profit/valuation card becomes a qualified scenario until the gap is resolved.

### What should run through Everstreet

The ambition is an operating platform that handles the meaningful inputs, not a dashboard that asks people to retype them. Support direct operation, connected sources and honest manual entry. The profile names which route each figure used.

| Flow or record | What Everstreet handles or connects | How it affects the profile |
|---|---|---|
| Customer payments / top-ups | Accepted payment purpose, asset, customer entitlement and settlement outcome | Customer receipts and prepaid liability; earned revenue only under the delivery rule |
| Usage credit | Purchase lot, accepted price, remaining funds, usage equivalent, holds and withdrawals | Unused customer credit and protected funds; aggregate publicly only if selected |
| API usage | Authorized request, result, price version, deduplication, metering/correction batch | Billable units, earned usage revenue, success/latency and cost per delivered unit |
| Fixed purchases, classes and services | Order, entitlement, delivery milestone, cancellation and remedy | Earned amounts for supported delivery periods; unfulfilled orders stay visible as obligations |
| Customer subscriptions | Plan, prepaid period or capped mandate, period delivery, cancellation and renewal state | Revenue for elapsed service, recurring run rate separately, unpaid/canceled/free accounts explicitly excluded as defined |
| Server and agent worker pay | Agreed rate, budget, accepted work, beneficiary, accrued payable and actual payout | Compute/delivery costs and worker payable; cash paid is distinct from cost incurred |
| People doing support or fulfillment | Approved hours or deliverables, invoice, reimbursement and pay state | Direct labor or overhead according to a declared policy; founder work still separately estimated if unpaid |
| Hosting, storage, bandwidth and upstream APIs | Usage invoices, subscriptions, supplier credits and payment observations | Direct delivery cost where attributable; vendor concentration and operational dependency |
| Software, monitoring and administration | Recurring vendor commitments, invoices and paid/unpaid status | Overhead; upcoming bills and missing invoice exceptions |
| Marketing and referrals | Approved spend, invoices, attribution and disclosed referral payments | Acquisition/marketing overhead and source of customers; attribution uncertainty retained |
| Platform, payment and network fees | Gross customer amount, provider fee, payer and settlement asset | Gross-to-net receipt bridge and separately classified cost; sponsor-paid fees do not silently become business expense |
| Refunds of unused credit | Original lot/beneficiary, pending claim, completed return and reason | Reduce cash and prepaid liability; do not subtract unearned credit from earned revenue again |
| Refunds or credits for delivered work | Original billed unit/period, reversal policy and actual financial result | Revenue correction or separately explained allowance; link the correction to the source period |
| Disputes, failed payments and chargebacks | Provider state, disputed amount, restricted funds and final outcome where that rail exists | Unresolved exposure and eventual correction; no supported-card claim until that integration exists |
| Discounts, trials and promotional credits | Funding source, paid/noncash distinction and redemption terms | Net earned price and separate trial usage; a promotional grant is not a cash sale or redeemable paid credit |
| External sales and expenses | Connected provider observation or dated supplied evidence, matching/deduplication and completeness declaration | Clearly tagged additional revenue/costs; never call all activity NEAR-verified because some payments are |
| Equipment, inventory and capital purchases | Asset record, stock/usage basis, replacement assumption and purchase payment | Category-specific earnings adjustments and transfer assets; avoid expensing and adding the same item twice in value |
| Taxes and pass-through amounts | Separately classified collection/payable and selected reporting policy | Excluded from operating revenue where collected for another party; liabilities and scope notes remain visible |
| Owner capital, debt, draws and transfers | Explicit funding/financing purpose and matched internal movements | Treasury and financing bridge; excluded from customer revenue and ordinary operating profit |
| Acquisition funding and sale proceeds | Separate buyer hold, accepted deal, seller claim and withdrawal | Deal settlement only; no revenue spike for the acquired business |
| Provider/service performance | Request traces, probe observations, incidents, support response and delivery outcomes | Operational evidence alongside financials; money movement is not service-performance verification |

Each category appears only when the integration or manual route is supported. This is the completeness map, not a requirement to ship every connector before the first payment. The first version needs payments, prepaid balances, one usage type, bounded worker pay, a few cost categories and refunds. A **Missing from your numbers** panel lists declared external activity, unclassified transactions, unreceived bills and estimated founder labor.

### The performance headline has a denominator

Paperfin's fictional month has **1,250,000 eligible customer requests**, of which **1,247,500 succeed**: **99.80%**. Of the successes, **1,200,000 are billable** and **47,500 use a separately recorded free allowance**. The 2,500 service failures are unbilled. Synthetic probes, invalid authentication and duplicate retries are separately counted, with their inclusion rules visible. Never infer revenue from the total success count.

Show **p95 successful-request latency: 184 ms**, measured by the connected gateway over that same period; retain the distribution and measurement boundary. This is not end-user network latency, an independent uptime measurement or a promise for every request. Include a separate probe-availability series only when there is a defined schedule, recorded expected/observed probes and disclosed gaps. A period with no traffic cannot establish availability by having no recorded failures.

An incident drawer shows duration, affected endpoints, customer impact, credited/refunded work, reporting source and resolution. Reducing errors by dropping difficult requests from the denominator should be visible through the exclusion categories. Release markers and ownership changes are chart annotations, not reasons to erase the previous period.

## 5. Owner rules are part of the product people buy

### A founder makes a small, understandable constitution

Call the public section **Owner rules**. Explain: **“These are promises the business makes to its customers. They also limit what its current and future owners can do.”** Give the founder an offer-specific starting set and show the operating consequences before publishing it.

Use a small supported vocabulary first. Each rule has a beneficiary, exact scope, effective period, change policy, enforcement source and remedy. The contract-facing representation is structured; the prose is a readable rendering of it. AI can explain or propose an option, but cannot turn arbitrary prose into an executable guarantee.

| Proposed rule | What the customer sees | What the buyer sees | Enforcement and product requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unused paid credit is yours to withdraw** | “Withdraw unused credit at any time. In-progress requests may hold up to their stated charge until [bounded expiry].” Show request status and payout time separately | Outstanding credit, pending withdrawals, asset and reserve coverage | Customer-authorized claim to the original beneficiary; no owner approval; no double spend with usage; async payment resolution. Requestable any time is not a promise of instantaneous network settlement |
| **Reserved funds cannot be withdrawn by an owner** | “Money reserved for unused paid credit stays protected.” | Available treasury after every registered customer claim, worker payable and pending movement; coverage as of a stated instant | Hard invariant across owner withdrawal, worker pay, fees, close and recovery. Spending through a worker cannot bypass the reserve |
| **Purchased usage keeps its accepted rate** | “Your existing purchased balance continues at the price shown on your receipt until used or refunded.” | Remaining balance by price version and the cost of serving it | Preserve accepted lot/entitlement economics. A new owner cannot devalue old credits by changing their conversion rate or adding a mandatory fee |
| **Existing prepaid customers keep their price for 90 days after a sale** | “If you qualify at the closing snapshot, new purchases of this same package keep your old price through [date].” Explain package, limits and cutoff | Eligible cohort, included package/volume limits, expiry and projected margin under the rule | Versioned eligibility and rate ceiling bound to the sale time; the old balance's protection may last longer. Changes to fees, minimum package or included output cannot evade the ceiling |
| **Failed renders are not billed** | “A failed render releases its charge hold. See or dispute a usage record.” | Failure policy, corrections, unpaid work and reporter trust | Service reports the result; contract enforces authorized amounts and correction/hold constraints. Label the delivery assertion as service-enforced unless there is stronger evidence |
| **Material price or feature changes get notice** | “New purchases change no earlier than [effective date]; your existing commitments remain.” | Notice period and versioned future offers; contracts still needing service | App/service enforces publication and acceptance version. If the contract also checks the effective time, identify that particular protection separately |
| **Customer funds have a supported closure route** | “If new orders stop, unused credit can still be reclaimed through [route].” | Wind-down responsibilities, service/data export and unresolved claims | Direct supported claim path, beneficiary authentication and asset/gas availability; product must exercise the route with the normal platform UI unavailable |
| **Service target and remedy** | A defined target, measurement window, exclusions and specified refund/credit remedy | Cost of the remedy, measurement source and any cap/reserve | Provider/service commitment unless a supported funding and trigger mechanism actually enforces the remedy. Do not issue a generic on-chain uptime guarantee |

The first three are my recommended default economic protections for the API. Reserve protection is not a toggle an owner can turn off. Add the 90-day post-sale price rule as a supported, explicit option, with Paperfin selecting it. Scope it carefully enough that the founder understands the continuing obligation. If unsupported in the deployed version, show it as a proposed commitment with its real enforcement, or withhold the option; never silently downgrade a promised guarantee.

The 90-day rule needs a precise answer to “90 days for whom and for what?” Recommended fixture: customers with positive paid credit at closing qualify for the same API package at $0.01 per successful billable render, for up to $1,000 of additional credit per eligible billing account across the 90 days. That permits 100,000 additional renders at the protected rate. The period is measured from the confirmed closing time. Already purchased credit keeps its original economics independently of that deadline; credit bought during the protected window does too. Show that continuing cost exposure to the founder and buyer. A later sale cannot shorten an existing protection; if it grants a later protection, show the later applicable date. Buyer and customer screens use the same eligibility and date calculation.

### Three literal enforcement labels

| Label | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Enforced by the business contract** | The deployed code denies the violating supported operation; list code/version, covered methods and any bypass authority | Owner cannot withdraw the registered unused-credit reserve |
| **Enforced by the service** | A named service/provider implements the behavior and supplies evidence; its host remains a trust dependency | Gateway deduplicates request retries and applies the supported billing/result rule |
| **Owner commitment** | Published promise with its specified contractual/support remedy; no technical enforcement claimed | Founder offers a scheduled transition call or an off-platform support period |

A single customer policy may have all three components. “Failed requests are free” can have contract-enforced spending bounds and a service-supplied success assertion. Show the boundary in one expandable explanation rather than giving the whole policy the strongest badge.

Display **Who can change this?** next to the rule, including code/upgrade control and all ways to bypass it. A keyless account alone is insufficient if its contract can redeploy itself or add a privileged key. NEAR's documentation distinguishes full-access powers from locked accounts whose contract may still contain such methods. The current repository's stated no-key, no-upgrade model is the intended baseline to preserve. [NEAR access-key and locked-account documentation](https://docs.near.org/protocol/accounts-contracts/access-keys)

### Publishing, revising and living with a rule

The founder flow is **Choose → See customer wording → See operating cost → Review enforcement → Publish for new orders**. For a price-protection rule, show a scenario with higher compute costs during the protected period. The point is to choose a promise the business can honor. A proposed extra week of protection is not necessarily free.

Existing receipts retain their accepted rules. A revision can improve protected rights or govern new purchases according to the supported policy; it cannot quietly weaken an old entitlement. Keep historical versions accessible to entitled customers and permitted buyers. A buyer accepting a sale acknowledges the continuing rules; buyer and seller cannot jointly vote away customer claims. Cooperative votes have the same constraint.

The rule view should also answer **What if the owner stops responding?** Refund and evidence access should not depend on a friendly message to the seller. The scope of the independent path must be explicit: a user may reclaim supported unused credit even though the chain cannot force a server to render a document. If service stops, the user sees the available remedy and its state.

### Why rules can make a business more valuable

A customer knows what they are trusting. A founder can build a lasting relationship instead of asking for blind faith. A buyer sees the actual constraints before pricing the business and can plan the first quarter of operation. Consistent, inspectable terms can reduce unanswered diligence questions and make a service more attractive to customers who depend on it.

Treat the value effect as a commercial hypothesis to test. Strong rules also create real liabilities and may constrain margins. The valuation model carries their projected costs and replacement needs; it does not apply a “trust premium” by fiat. Useful evidence would be fewer customer objections, repeat usage, shorter diligence on obligations and successful post-sale fulfillment.

**Needs / size:** structured supported rules, plain-language renderer, version/eligibility model, customer receipt binding, rule amendment constraints, source inspection and buyer liability projection; L in the product plus material contract work. Cut the arbitrary rule builder first. Keep a few promises that are intelligible, testable and actually enforced.

## 6. The social layer: a business worth following

### Follow the business's progress and usefulness

Keep the launch-pad surfaces **Starting now**, **Open for customers**, **What people need**, and **Following**. The living profile becomes their shared destination. A follow belongs to an account and a business, independent of the founder's continuing ownership. Following Nadia personally is a separate choice.

On Follow, offer **Product and availability**, **Building the business**, and, where published, **Numbers and milestones**. A cooperative can later add **Public governance updates**. Default to an in-app chronological digest that can be muted or unfollowed. Required service notices and private customer receipts have their own delivery basis; they must not depend on following marketing updates.

An update has a useful unit of substance: **what happened, what supports it, what someone can do next**. Examples:

- “Paperfin added a receipt template after three support requests. Try the sample.” The post links to the release and only consented/aggregated evidence.
- “October's books: $12,000 earned; $4,500 operating costs. Here is the missing cost we found.” The posted card freezes that disclosed version and retains its fictional/production source label.
- “A second operator restored Paperfin without Nadia. Here is the revised runbook.” The public excerpt omits private configuration and points to the dated rehearsal summary.
- “Juniper opened another Saturday workshop after its first session. Book one of the actual remaining places.” Workshop availability remains a real action, not merely a social announcement.

AI drafts a **monthly business letter** from approved public fields and asks the founder to add what they learned. It cannot read a private buyer room to produce a public update. Founder approval publishes narrative. An already authorized live metric card can refresh from its defined projection without asking the founder to retype numbers.

### Milestones that mean something

Offer candidate milestones such as **First fulfilled paid order**, **First repeat customer**, **First completed operating month**, **Second operator completed a shift**, **First tested restore**, and **Customer-owned** after actual confirmed group control. Each has a definition, event date and evidence source. “First 50 customer accounts” must explain its account definition and exclusions; authenticated accounts do not prove 50 independent people.

Do not automatically publish a revenue milestone or sale announcement. Respect the previously selected audience, invite review where required and show the exact card. Deleted promotional posts need not remain forever, but removing a post must not rewrite an accepted financial snapshot, confirmed ownership history or a customer's receipt.

### Reactions, discovery and incentives

My recommendation is **no public like counts, follower leaderboards or valuation rankings at launch**. Offer Save, Follow, Share, Try, Ask and a private **Useful, thank you** response to the author. Where discussion is useful, start with a focused question and moderated replies. Verified purchase/activity linkage can qualify a review, but does not prove independence or a favorable experience.

Discovery should ask what the visitor wants: a useful API, a workshop this weekend, another founder solving a similar problem, or a business open to acquisition. Match the corresponding evidence. Revenue and valuation can be authorized acquisition filters without becoming the public network's status hierarchy. Let a new founder earn attention through a sample, a clear promise and responsiveness before they have impressive numbers.

Keep paid placement out of organic recommendations. Label related-party activity, owner purchases, free allowances and tests. Limit repeated promotional posts and automated replies; provide report, block and correction routes. A founder cannot buy a stronger evidence label. Public metrics need a visible period and a standard definition so a business cannot silently call a good week its monthly performance.

Measure social usefulness through responses that lead to trials, fulfilled orders, relevant advice, returning customers and resolved operating problems. Reading a monthly report may be valuable without a purchase. Follower growth and reactions alone cannot establish business health.

**Needs / size:** business/person follow distinction, topic preferences, audience-safe event/post projections, frozen metric embeds, notification policy, reporting/moderation and consented references; M for follows and updates, L for a healthy shared network. Start chronological and curated. Cut trending algorithms, public reaction counts and unrestricted unsolicited messaging.

## 9. Requirements for Claude's contract and service design

At the opening read, **docs/design/BUSINESS_V2.md did not exist**. I have not waited for it or inferred its contents. The following are product requirements for Claude to reconcile with that design. They are not proposed edits to the contract, an assertion that v2 already supports them, or a prescribed Rust interface.

### The contract has a bounded job inside a larger business

The proposed split is:

~~~text
Business account on NEAR
  owner and generation / supported customer money / economic rules
  scoped worker authority and pay claims / exact managed-rights sale
                |
                +-- Operating service
                |     stable endpoint, customer authentication, API results
                |     bounded usage authorizations, worker execution
                |     runtime, backups, release artifacts, service evidence
                |
                +-- Platform records and projections
                      profile, approved story, permissioned social activity
                      source classification, financial/performance snapshots
                      estimate, evidence rooms and external transfer items
~~~

Brand belongs in UI and docs. Account-independent business IDs, schema fields, events and contract methods remain brand-neutral. A source/receipt hash proves which record a calculation used, not whether the underlying off-chain claim was true.

| Requirement | Product consequence | Must exist underneath / scope |
|---|---|---|
| **R1. Independent business authority** | “You own the business; your successor can operate it.” Business administration follows the actual current owner | One supported control account/generation, independently controlled signer, explicit recovery model and inspection of code/key bypasses. Profile admin cannot be a permanent founder flag; foundational |
| **R2. Separate kinds of money** | A customer sees credit, an owner sees available cash, and a seller sees proceeds without ambiguous totals | Per-asset purpose classification and balance/reserve invariants for customer funds, usage holds, worker payables, buyer funds, seller claims and pending payouts. One supported asset first; foundational |
| **R3. Actual accepted payments** | Funding creates credit only after the supported payment succeeds | Validate allowed token contract, original sender, amount, purpose and operation state; recognize actual outcome, deduplicate and reconcile. Demo issuance is its own mode; foundational |
| **R4. Customer-controlled exit** | A customer can reclaim unused, unheld paid credit without either owner's consent, including after a sale | Authorized beneficiary claim; race-safe usage/refund accounting; bounded holds, exactly-once final state and recoverable async payout; foundational |
| **R5. Preserved economic terms** | The same customer balance cannot be revalued or diluted by a new owner | Accepted price/rule versions, precise protection eligibility and effective times; limits enforced from structured fields where promised. A hash of prose alone is insufficient; foundational |
| **R6. Bounded metering** | API calls can be fast without giving a worker permission to empty every account | Customer-authorized ceilings/holds, request or batch identity, reporter authority, accepted rate, replay prevention, corrections and retained private evidence. Explain attestation trust and bound outstanding unfinalized usage; L–XL |
| **R7. Workers do work and earn pay** | The business can appoint servers and people, show what they cost and pay them | Capability/resource/expiry/budget limits, beneficiary and pay agreement, accepted work, accrual and payout state. A worker cannot invent a payable or consume reserved customer money; L |
| **R8. Successor team prepared safely** | Customers can keep using the API while privileged control changes | Buyer-authorized next-generation plan committed to the deal; no early buyer power; no surviving discretionary seller grant; service barrier, readiness freshness and reconciled in-flight work. This is beyond today's documented gate-1 finality window; L–XL |
| **R9. Existing commitments survive revocation** | An old-generation approved refund or payable still reaches its rightful recipient once | Separate discretionary authority from fulfillment of already fixed commitments; immutable beneficiary/amount bounds and idempotent callback/reconciliation state. Blindly rejecting every old-generation callback would strand claims; foundational |
| **R10. Exact, fundable sale scope** | Buyer and seller know which control, cash, rules and assets the accepted price covers | Versioned accepted manifest/digest, price/asset, parties, expiry, generation, funding and live invariant checks. Min working cash, protected claims, material rule changes and successor plan included where agreed; foundational |
| **R11. Live service need not freeze its customer ledger** | Usage can continue while a deal is reviewed | Commit the liability policy and closing constraints, then evaluate the actual protected balances at close. Do not require reacceptance for every normal call, or freeze customer withdrawals merely to keep a screenshot unchanged. Material changes outside accepted bounds require new terms; L |
| **R12. Group owner is real authority** | A cooperative, not an employee with a shared password, can own and operate the business | A group-controlled owner account that enforces its approval rules and can call the supported methods; versioned proposal/expiry handling and later operator governance. An arbitrary group-owner token is unnecessary; L |
| **R13. Performance reports identify their reporter** | A buyer can distinguish observed service success from chain settlement | Authorized service reports, covered window/denominator, gaps, collection boundary, correction history and optional independent probes. Store necessary commitments compactly, keep private payloads off chain; M–L |
| **R14. Evidence can be reproduced and disclosed narrowly** | Public profile, owner desk and buyer room agree without disclosing the same raw material | Paginated/indexable events, source identity, reconciliation state, versioned metric/valuation manifests, purpose/category/expiry grants and safe exports. Customer metadata stays protected; L |
| **R15. The active version's limitations stay visible** | A business cannot gain an unsupported policy merely because the UI has a new checkbox | Capability/version discovery, deployment lineage and an explicit future migration/adoption design. Existing v1 is not silently upgraded and its obligations are not moved by a profile pointer; foundational |

NEAR's FT receiver guidance distinguishes the calling token contract from the initiating sender and requires expected-token validation; this is the protocol basis for R3, not a claim that the new payment integration has been built. [NEAR fungible-token receiver guidance](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/security/cross-contract-calls/ft-transfer-call)

One final matching acceptance can change internal claims and control within one business contract execution. Token payout, group-account calls, host changes and external provider work have their own outcomes. NEAR cross-contract calls are asynchronous and do not globally roll back prior successful work. The UI must preserve those states and recover a failed payout without repeating the sale. [NEAR cross-contract execution](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/anatomy/crosscontract)

Extend the launch-pad model with brand-neutral conceptual records for **UsageAuthorization**, **UsageSettlement**, **CreditLot**, **RuleVersion**, **WorkerAgreement**, **WorkerPayable**, **PerformanceWindow**, **Incident**, **Dependency**, **ContinuityPlan**, **ProfilePublication**, **FollowPreference** and **PeriodDisclosure**. A daily estimate need not be written on chain just to make the profile feel live.

High-volume APIs should not require one blockchain transaction and a fresh interactive wallet approval per request. The strongest acceptance observations for the proposed product are:

2. A customer prepays, receives a usable result, sees the correct remaining balance and exercises a refund under the accepted terms.
3. A viewer can reproduce the $13,000 receipt / $12,000 revenue / $7,500 profit / $9,000 available-funds distinctions from the fictional records, and can see the original units/source labels in a test run.

## 12. Six questions for Tony, with my defaults

These are product decisions to discuss from this concrete proposal, not reasons to stop the authorized design or the reversible platform work.

1. **Should the headline pair be “Start your business here” and “Built to change hands intact”?** My recommendation: **yes**. Keep creation as the invitation, and use continuity as the reason the business is structurally different from its first paid day. Let “Build something worth owning” remain the growth language.
2. **Should the profitable API become the flagship for the complete handover?** My recommendation: **yes**, with a small self-contained service like Paperfin. Keep OpenSeat and Juniper as equally real parts of the platform, and use them to show that founders and customers extend beyond developers. This adds an example; it does not erase the original community or gate-1 work.
3. **How much should a founder be encouraged to publish about the business?** My recommendation: **make numbers a prominent, proudly designed option, with a private default and a clear “Share my numbers” preview**. Offer public bands or monthly summaries, follower updates and precise scoped buyer access. Never infer acquisition intent from financial openness.
4. **Which owner rules should be standard?** My recommendation: **unused-credit withdrawal, protected reserves and preservation of purchased terms**. Give Paperfin the explicit 90-day post-sale price-protection option too. Prefer a small set of rules the deployed system can enforce over an open-ended promise editor.
5. **How far should customer-cooperative ownership go in the first product?** My recommendation: **make the group-owner end state real after the ordinary close works**, using a demonstrated group-controlled account.
