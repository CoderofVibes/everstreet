## Tony's words (Nov 1), in full

On keeping the original idea:

> One thing I want to add is I don't want us to lose the original idea of this being a way to
> securely transfer a business during its purchase: from customers and products to treasury,
> because it's all on NEAR. Here's what I don't want to lose:
>
> "Imagine a developer who operates a profitable API and wants to move on. Today, selling a small
> SaaS business can mean moving hosting, billing, customer access, source code, and credentials
> through separate handoffs. Acquire.com's own transfer guide reads like a checklist of places the
> business can break.
>
> Going Concern [now Everstreet] is a platform for creating internet businesses that can change
> hands intact. A founder launches a paid service with its own NEAR account. The business holds
> its treasury, tracks prepaid customer balances, pays the workers that deliver its service, and
> publishes the rules governing what a future owner may change. A prospective buyer can inspect
> revenue, operating costs, outstanding obligations, and actual service performance before buying
> it. A customer cooperative could buy the service it depends on."

On numbers and profiles:

> Real revenue, performance numbers, overhead, profit, valuation estimated by Everstreet [is a big
> thing for me]. We can have so much run through Everstreet, and I'm envisioning all of this being
> like a business social media (for lack of a better term). It onboards aspiring entrepreneurs,
> they create their internet business in there and it helps, it integrates them onto NEAR, their
> financials and valuation is part of their profile (profiles could be business overview,
> performance, customers, about me's, financials, valuations, basically a whole generated profile
> that Everstreet can help generate and maintain for them).

On order of work:

> Building the platform, then examples, further building out the platform, then once we have a
> good product, finalizing what we give to the judges.

## What we need from you

1. **"Born to change hands intact" as the thread through the whole product.** How it shows up
   while a founder launches (their business gets its own NEAR account, treasury and published
   owner rules from day one, explained simply), while it runs, and when it sells. Why this makes
   Everstreet different from a launch tool plus a sale listing.
2. **The flagship internet business, end to end:** Tony's developer with a profitable API.
   Launch on Everstreet, customers prepay usage credits, workers (servers and people) that deliver
   the API are appointed and paid by the business, owner rules published, revenue/costs/
   performance accumulate, a buyer inspects them, the sale closes, and customers keep calling the
   API without noticing. Then the same story with a **customer cooperative** buying the API it
   depends on. Keep workshops and other businesses in the product; this is the example where the
   handover can be most complete.
3. **The living business profile ("business social media").** The profile sections Tony named
   (overview, performance, customers, about me, financials, valuation) and any others worth
   adding. For each: what Everstreet generates and keeps current automatically from what runs
   through it, what the founder writes, what AI drafts, how fresh it is, who can see it (public,
   followers, verified buyers, private), and how a customer, a fellow founder and an acquirer
   each read it. The social layer: following, updates, milestones, discovery, reactions or not,
   and how it stays about real businesses rather than hype.
4. **Real numbers computed by Everstreet:** revenue, overhead, profit, performance and the
   Everstreet valuation estimate on the profile. Make it a headline. List everything that could
   run through Everstreet so the numbers are complete (customer payments, prepaid credits, API
   usage metering, worker pay, expenses, subscriptions, refunds) and how each shows on the
   profile. Keep your honest source labels, compactly.
5. **Owner rules, as a product:** which rules a founder can publish ("prepaid customers keep
   their price for 90 days after a sale", "customers can withdraw unused credit any time",
   "reserved funds cannot be withdrawn"), how customers and buyers see them, and why they make a
   business more valuable.
6. **UI:** wireframes for the business profile (public and buyer view), the financials and
   performance sections, the owner-rules view, and the "what transfers" view in a deal.
7. **Build order update:** fold this into your eight phases following Tony's order (platform,
   then examples, more platform, judges last). Say which phases change.
8. **A few questions for Tony** with your recommendation.
